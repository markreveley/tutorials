Appendix B

Projection and SVD explorers

Two-dimensional pictures of the two central operations. Drag the handles or type coordinates; the numbers are computed locally.

Projection onto a direction

The blue arrow is v, the dashed line is the direction d, the orange segment is the projection of v onto d and the red dashed segment is the residual. Drag either handle, or type coordinates. Start with v = (3, 4) and d = (1, 1) to match the hand computation in lesson 03: coefficient 7/√2 ≈ 4.95, projection (3.5, 3.5), residual (−0.5, 0.5). Then rotate d until the residual vanishes, and until the coefficient turns negative.

The coefficient does not change when d is scaled; the dot product v·d does. The residual is always orthogonal to d, and the squared lengths of projection and residual add up to the squared length of v.

A 2×2 matrix as a map

Enter the four entries of a matrix A = [[a, b], [c, d]] in the row-vector convention x ↦ x A, so the first row is where (1, 0) goes. The dashed unit circle is the set of inputs of length 1; the shaded ellipse is where the matrix sends them. The dashed arrows are the right singular vectors v₁, v₂ (the input directions), and the orange arrows σ₁u₁ and σ₂u₂ are the ellipse’s semi-axes (the output directions, stretched). Try [[3, 0], [0, 2]] for a pure stretch, [[0, −1], [1, 0]] for a rotation (both singular values 1), [[1, 2], [2, 4]] for a rank-one matrix whose ellipse collapses to a line, and [[2, 1], [1, 2]] for a symmetric matrix whose singular vectors are its eigenvectors. See lesson 12.

The product of the singular values equals the absolute determinant, the area scale factor. A zero singular value means the matrix has rank one and loses a dimension. The explorer uses the closed form through AᵀA; the lab uses np.linalg.svd.
