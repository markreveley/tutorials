Module 1 · Linear maps

Span, linear independence, and rank

How many directions does a set of vectors really contain? Rank answers it for a matrix, and in 768 dimensions the answer to “how many nearly-orthogonal directions fit” is far more than 768.

Span and independence

The span of a set of vectors is everything you can reach by linear combinations of them: a line for one nonzero vector, a plane for two vectors not on the same line, and in general a flat through the origin called a subspace. The vectors are linearly independent if none is a combination of the others, equivalently if the only combination that gives the zero vector has all coefficients zero. Three vectors in the plane are never independent; two vectors on the same line are not; (1, 0) and (1, 1) are. The dimension of a subspace is the number of independent vectors needed to span it, and a basis of a subspace is any such set. The 3Blue1Brown chapter on span and basis vectors is the picture for this paragraph.

Rank

The rank of a matrix is the number of linearly independent columns, which always equals the number of independent rows. Read as a map, rank is the dimension of the space the map can reach: a (768, 64) matrix sends ℝ⁷⁶⁸ into a subspace of ℝ⁶⁴ of dimension at most 64, and a (768, 768) matrix formed as a product through a 64-dimensional space can reach at most a 64-dimensional subspace of ℝ⁷⁶⁸, however large its grid of numbers looks. Read as a batch of row vectors, rank is how many directions the rows actually span.

rank(AB) ≤ min(rank A, rank B)
rank of an (n, d) matrix ≤ min(n, d)
numerically: count singular values above a tolerance (lesson 12)

In floating point, “independent” has to mean “independent beyond rounding”, so numpy counts singular values larger than a tolerance, which is the method the exercise asks you to implement. The lab reports three ranks from the fixture.

python3 labs/linalg_lab.py rank

rank of W_QK = W_Q @ W_K.T (768, 768): 64;  rank of W_OV = W_V @ W_O (768, 768): 64  (a product through 64 dimensions has rank at most 64)
768 random unit vectors in 768 dims: rank 768; max |cosine| between any two 0.169
2000 random unit vectors in 768 dims: rank 768; max |cosine| between any two 0.196
5000 random unit vectors in 768 dims: rank 768; max |cosine| between any two 0.189
three 4-D vectors where the third is v0 + 2 v1: rank 2

Three readings. The activation matrix has rank 302: its 302 rows are independent, which with 768 dimensions available is unsurprising; they span a 302-dimensional subspace of ℝ⁷⁶⁸, and anything you compute from this fixture lives in that subspace. The two weight products have rank exactly 64 despite being 768×768: the first bound in the formula box, with the 64-dimensional head space as the bottleneck. The framework paper makes this the central fact about attention heads, that W_Q W_Kᵀ and W_V W_O “can always be thought of as individual, low-rank matrices”, and for the full vocabulary-to-vocabulary versions, “They are 50,000 × 50,000 matrices, but only rank d_head (64 or 128).” [Elhage et al. 2021] Lesson 12 looks inside them with the SVD.

More directions than dimensions

The last three lines before the toy example answer a question that sounds paradoxical. In 768 dimensions at most 768 vectors can be mutually orthogonal, and indeed the rank never exceeds 768. But the largest cosine between any two of 5,000 random unit vectors is 0.189, and it barely grew from 768 to 2,000 to 5,000 vectors. Thousands of directions fit in 768 dimensions if you only require them to be nearly orthogonal, and the count grows very fast with the dimension. The Toy Models paper states the mathematical fact this way: “Although it’s only possible to have n orthogonal vectors in an n-dimensional space, it’s possible to have exp(n) many ‘almost orthogonal’ (< ε cosine similarity) vectors in high-dimensional spaces.” [Elhage et al. 2022]

That geometric fact is the whole of what this course says about superposition. The same paper defines the term: models “represent more features than they have dimensions. We call this phenomenon superposition”, and later, “Superposition simply means that there are more features than dimensions.” [Elhage et al. 2022] Whether GPT-2’s residual stream holds features in superposition, which ones, and how many are empirical questions that this course does not answer and the fixture cannot. What the fixture shows is only the geometry that makes the question possible: a 768-dimensional space has room for far more than 768 distinguishable directions, at the price that projecting onto one of them picks up a little of each of the others (the cosine 0.19 above is that price, for random directions). The ARENA exercise set on superposition and SAEs replicates the paper’s toy models if you want the empirical side. [ARENA]

What rank buys you

If a batch of activations has rank r, then r directions suffice to describe it exactly, and PCA (lesson 11) finds the best r′ < r of them. If a weight product has rank 64, then its action on the 768-dimensional stream factors through 64 numbers, and the SVD (lesson 12) names the 64 input directions it reads and the 64 output directions it writes. If a set of probe directions has rank lower than its count, some of them are combinations of others and are not separate findings. Rank is the first thing to compute about any matrix whose size suggests more structure than it has.

In interpretability work

“The head writes to a 64-dimensional subspace of the residual stream” is a rank statement. “These 300 features are all distinct” is a rank-and-cosine statement, and rank alone cannot settle it: 300 random directions in 768 dimensions have rank 300 and pairwise cosines around 0.1 to 0.2, so distinctness needs a stricter criterion than independence.

Pitfall

Trusting a rank computed in float32 on a product. Rounding noise at the 10⁻⁶ level produced a numerical rank of 768 for W_QK in an early version of the lab; computing the product in float64 gives 64. Check the tolerance and the dtype before believing a rank.

Check your understanding

The activation matrix has rank 302, the full count of its rows. Does that mean the 302 tokens are “all different” in any interesting sense?

Reveal the reasoning

No. In 768 dimensions, any 302 vectors that were not deliberately constructed to be dependent will be independent; a rank of 302 is the generic outcome and carries almost no information. It would be interesting only if the rank were much lower than 302, which would mean the activations lie in a small subspace. The informative version of the question is how much of the variance a few directions explain, which is PCA, not rank.

Practice

Rerun and read the maximum cosine among the random vectors. Then compute by hand the cosine of (1, 1, 0) and (1, 0, 1), two of the many “nearly orthogonal” directions in three dimensions.

python3 labs/linalg_lab.py rank
What to look for

The cosine is 1/(√2·√2) = 0.5: in three dimensions you cannot do better than that with vectors of this simple form, and with a tolerance of 0.5 you still cannot fit many. The random-vector cosines of about 0.19 are only possible because 768 is large; the number of nearly orthogonal directions grows exponentially with the dimension, which is the fact the superposition literature builds on.
