Module 4 · Calculus and rigor

Readiness review

Given a cached activation matrix and a label, find a direction, test it on tokens you did not use, run PCA and SVD, and say what each number means. The rubric is what a reviewer would ask.

The task

You are handed an activation matrix X of shape (n, d), a label y of length n, and the weight matrices of one component. In one sitting, without the lessons open, you should be able to do the following and narrate it. (1) Report shapes and norms, and decide which rows to exclude and why. (2) Split the tokens, fit a least-squares (or ridge) direction on one part, and report its held-out correlation. (3) Report a permutation baseline and a random-direction baseline for that correlation. (4) Run PCA on the training rows, report variance explained for the first few components, and state the cosine between your direction and the top component. (5) Remove your direction from the activations, refit, and report what remains readable. (6) Run the SVD of a weight product, report its rank and the shape of its singular value spectrum. (7) For every number, say in one sentence what it measures and what it does not establish. The lab’s review command runs steps 2 to 5 end to end on the fixture.

python3 labs/linalg_lab.py review --label is_digit
readiness run on is_digit: 211 train, 90 held out
1 direction by least squares: |w| 0.0374; held-out correlation 0.986
2 permutation baseline: 97.5th percentile 0.219
3 PCA on train: variance explained [0.075 0.061 0.055 0.041 0.036]; correlation of each component score with is_digit on held out: 0.191 0.517 -0.145 0.738 -0.124
4 cos(w, PCA component 1) = 0.055
5 after removing w: refit held-out correlation 0.696

A model narration

“The least-squares direction w, fitted on 211 of the 301 non-outlier tokens, predicts the digit label on the other 90 with correlation 0.986. Shuffling the label gives at most about 0.22 at the 97.5th percentile, and lesson 14’s random directions reached 0.62, so the direction is both related to the label and far better than chance geometry. PCA on the training rows has a slow scree (7.5%, 6.1%, 5.5%, …), and no single component is the digit direction: the best, component 4, correlates 0.738 with the label on held-out tokens, and w has cosine 0.055 with component 1, so the probe is nearly orthogonal to the dominant variation, which lesson 11 found to track position. Removing w from every token and refitting leaves a held-out correlation of 0.696: the label remains partly linearly readable from the remaining 767 dimensions, so w is a direction that reads digit-ness, not the only one, and the claim ‘the digit information lives along w’ is not supported. Nothing here says what the model does with w.”

Notice what the narration does at each step: it names the sample the number came from, the baseline it was compared with, and the stronger claim it declines to make. That is the competence the course is after.

What a tutor may ask instead

The fixture is public, so a tutor assessing readiness will vary it. The families of variants, described but not published, per the assessment contract: a different passage through the same layer; a different layer of the same passage; a label the fixture does not include, computed from the tokens; a different attention head’s weights, possibly one with a steep singular spectrum; an activation matrix with the outlier row left in without warning; a label that is a confound of another (every digit preceded by the same word); and a direction supplied by the tutor rather than fitted, to be tested rather than found. The commands in this course answer all of them with no new code, which is the point.

In interpretability work

Every item in the rubric is a question you will be asked, or should ask, about a published direction. The ones most often missing from write-ups are the random-direction baseline and the post-removal refit.

Pitfall

Treating a passed rubric as a finding about GPT-2. The fixture is 301 tokens of one passage at one layer; it demonstrates the moves, not any property of the model. The findings come from the next two courses, with more data and a prediction written down first.

Check your understanding

Step 5 of the review removes w and the refitted probe still reaches 0.696, while lesson 08 removed the mean-difference direction and the refit fell to −0.053. Both directions read the digit label well before removal. Why can two good directions behave so differently under removal?

Reveal the reasoning

Because “reads the label” and “carries the label’s linearly readable variance” are different properties. The mean-difference direction points along the offset between the class means; removing it collapses the means together, and on this fixture little readable signal survives in other directions. The least-squares w, with 768 parameters and 211 rows, is one of infinitely many exact fits and is partly shaped by noise directions of small variance; removing it takes out that particular combination, and the class offset remains largely intact along other directions, which a refit finds. Removal tests a specific direction; it does not test a concept, and the two outcomes are consistent with each other.

Practice

Run the review on the after-period label and write the narration yourself, one sentence per numbered line, each ending with what the number does not establish.

python3 labs/linalg_lab.py review --label after_period
What to look for

Your held-out correlation will be around 0.7, the permutation percentile similar to the digit case, no component will dominate, and the post-removal refit will tell you whether sentence-initial information is spread across directions on this fixture. The narration is the deliverable; the numbers are its evidence.
