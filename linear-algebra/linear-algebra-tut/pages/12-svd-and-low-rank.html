SVD and low-rank structure

Every matrix is a rotation, a stretch along orthogonal axes, and another rotation. The singular values are the stretch factors, and keeping the largest few is the best low-rank approximation there is.

U Σ Vᵀ

The singular value decomposition writes any matrix A of shape (n, d) as A = U Σ Vᵀ, where V has orthonormal columns in the input space ℝᵈ, U has orthonormal columns in the output space ℝⁿ, and Σ is diagonal with non-negative entries σ₁ ≥ σ₂ ≥ … called singular values. Reading it as a map applied to a row vector x: Vᵀ measures x along the input directions (coordinates in an orthonormal basis, lesson 06), Σ scales each coordinate by σᵢ, U places the result along the output directions. Nothing is lost and nothing mixes: input direction vᵢ goes to σᵢ times output direction uᵢ. The browser SVD explorer draws this for a 2×2 matrix as the image of the unit circle, an ellipse whose semi-axes are σ₁ u₁ and σ₂ u₂.

A = U Σ Vᵀ = Σᵢ σᵢ uᵢ vᵢᵀ (a sum of rank-one matrices)
A vᵢ = σᵢ uᵢ; AᵀA vᵢ = σᵢ² vᵢ; rank(A) = number of nonzero σᵢ
best rank-k approximation: Aₖ = Σᵢ≤ₖ σᵢ uᵢ vᵢᵀ, with ‖A − Aₖ‖² = Σᵢ>ₖ σᵢ²

Three consequences follow from the formula box. The singular values squared are the eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix AᵀA, so the SVD is the eigendecomposition of lesson 10 for a matrix that need not be square or symmetric. The rank is the number of nonzero singular values, which is how lesson 07 counted it. And truncating the sum after k terms gives the closest rank-k matrix to A in the sum-of-squares sense, with the error accounted for exactly by the dropped singular values; this is the Eckart–Young result, and the lab verifies the accounting numerically rather than proving it.

Exactly 64 singular values are nonzero; the 65th onward are zero to the printed precision. The (768, 768) matrix is a sum of 64 rank-one pieces σᵢ uᵢ vᵢᵀ, and the rank-64 approximation reproduces it with zero error because there is nothing beyond 64 to drop. Within the 64, the spectrum is flat: the top value is 21.0 and the eighth is still above 16, so the rank-8 truncation leaves 85% of the matrix behind. This head’s query-key map uses its 64 dimensions fairly evenly; a head with one dominant singular value would be nearly a rank-one bilinear form, reading one input direction against one other. Which kind a head is, you now know how to find out.

What the directions mean is stated as geometry: the right singular vectors vᵢ of W_QK are the directions in the residual stream of the query token that the map responds to, the left singular vectors uᵢ are the directions in the key token’s stream they are compared against, and σᵢ is how strongly. The framework paper describes the SVD of W_OV in the same terms, right singular vectors for the subspace “read in” and left singular vectors for the subspace written to. [Elhage et al. 2021] That is why the paper treats the low-rank products, rather than the four factor matrices, as the objects to study: the factors can be changed without changing the product, but the product’s SVD is a property of the head. The transformers course does the circuits; this lesson only shows that the SVD is the right tool for a map that is a product through a bottleneck.

The SVD of the activation matrix

The second half of the output is the SVD of the centred activations, and its right singular vectors are the PCA components (cosine 1.000000 with component 1). The reconstruction-error lines are the Eckart–Young accounting on real data: the relative error of the rank-10 reconstruction is 0.7846, and the square root of the dropped singular values’ share of the total is also 0.7846, on every line. The error is not small: ten components reconstruct the tokens to within 78% relative error, consistent with the slow scree of lesson 11. At rank 301 nothing is dropped, because 301 centred rows have at most 301 nonzero singular values (in fact at most 300, since centring removes one), and the error is zero.

Computing it

numpy.linalg.svd returns U, the singular values in descending order, and Vᵀ (not V); with full_matrices=False the shapes are (n, k), (k,), (k, d) with k = min(n, d), and (U * S) @ Vt reconstructs the matrix. [numpy: svd] Keep the descending order and the transposed V in mind: the first row of Vt is the top right singular vector.

In interpretability work

Whenever a matrix is a product through a smaller dimension (head products, low-rank adapters, factorised embeddings), its SVD lists what it reads, what it writes, and how much, in rank order. Whenever a matrix is data, its SVD is PCA. The same decomposition, two readings; the singular value spectrum tells you in both cases whether a few directions carry the action.

Pitfall

Reading U and V from the wrong side. In the row-vector convention x ↦ x A, the input directions are the rows of Vᵀ and the output directions the columns of U; in the column convention A x they swap roles. Check with A vᵢ = σᵢ uᵢ on one vector before interpreting either.

Check your understanding

W_QK has 64 nonzero singular values with the top at 21.0 and the eighth above 16. If a different head had singular values 40, 2, 1, 0.5, … what could you say about it without any further information?

Reveal the reasoning

That its action on the residual stream is nearly rank one: it responds to one input direction v₁ and compares it against one output direction u₁ with weight 40, and every other pairing is at least twenty times weaker. A rank-one bilinear form q · k = 40 (x_q · v₁)(x_k · u₁) is a single projection on each side multiplied together, and the way to interpret the head would be to project tokens onto v₁ and u₁ and read the scalars, lesson 03 twice. The flat spectrum of the fixture head means no such shortcut exists for it.
