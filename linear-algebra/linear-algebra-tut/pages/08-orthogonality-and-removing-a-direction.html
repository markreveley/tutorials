Module 2 · Subspaces and fitting

Orthogonality, Gram–Schmidt, and removing a direction

To take a direction out of every activation you subtract its projection. Doing that for several directions in turn is Gram–Schmidt, and what remains is not what you might hope.

Orthogonal and orthonormal

Two vectors are orthogonal when their dot product is zero. A set is orthonormal when every vector has norm 1 and every pair is orthogonal. Orthonormal sets are the easy case for everything in this course: coordinates are dot products (lesson 06), projections onto different members do not interfere, and the projection onto the subspace they span is the sum of the projections onto each member. For a general set of directions none of that holds; project onto d₁ and then onto d₂, and if d₁ · d₂ ≠ 0 the second projection partly undoes the first.

Gram–Schmidt

Gram–Schmidt turns any list of vectors into an orthonormal list with the same span. Take the first vector and normalise it. Take the second, subtract its projection onto the first, normalise what is left. Take the third, subtract its projections onto the first two, normalise. If a vector is a combination of the earlier ones, subtracting the projections leaves (up to rounding) zero, and it is dropped: the procedure also discovers dependence. The lab runs it on three directions where the third is deliberately a combination of the first two.

q₁ = v₁ / ‖v₁‖
w₂ = v₂ − (v₂ · q₁) q₁, q₂ = w₂ / ‖w₂‖
wₖ = vₖ − Σⱼ<ₖ (vₖ · qⱼ) qⱼ, qₖ = wₖ / ‖wₖ‖ (drop if ‖wₖ‖ ≈ 0)

In floating point a single pass leaves small non-orthogonality; the reference implementation subtracts the projections twice, which is a standard remedy. The test checks Q Qᵀ = I and that the original vectors are reproduced from their coordinates on Q.

Removing a direction from activations

To remove direction d from a vector x, subtract the projection: x − (x · d̂) d̂. As a matrix, that is x (I − d̂ d̂ᵀ), the second of the three maps from lesson 04. Applied to every row of X at once, X − np.outer(X @ d̂, d̂), it produces an activation matrix in which every token has exactly zero component along d. This operation appears in the literature under several names (projecting out, ablating a direction, nulling), and its geometry is always this one line. What it does is precise; what it means for the model is not, and the lab is built to show the gap.

python3 labs/linalg_lab.py remove --label is_digit
direction d from the 211 training tokens; before: held-out correlation of projection on d with is_digit: 0.935
after removing d from every token: max |projection on d| = 2.13e-14; norms shrink from median 85.00 to 83.88
refit the mean-difference direction on the edited training tokens: |d2| = 1.89e-14 (was 36.45); the class means now coincide
ridge least-squares probe refitted on the edited activations: held-out correlation -0.053  (compare the probe on the unedited activations: 0.986)
gram_schmidt on [d, d_after_period, d + 0.5 d_after_period]: kept 2 orthonormal vectors; Q @ Q.T =
[[1. 0.]
 [0. 1.]]

Line by line. The mean-difference digit direction, built on 211 training tokens, has held-out correlation 0.935 with the digit label before anything is removed. After removal, every token’s component along d is zero to fourteen decimal places, and the median norm has dropped only from 85.00 to 83.88: the direction carried a small fraction of each vector’s length, as lesson 03 found for token 12. Refitting the mean-difference direction on the edited activations gives a vector of norm 10⁻¹⁴, which is an identity rather than a discovery: the difference of class means was d, and d has been removed, so the difference of class means is now zero. The fourth line is the real test. A least-squares probe (lesson 09) fitted on the edited activations reaches held-out correlation −0.053, where on the unedited activations it reached 0.986. On this fixture, for this label, removing the mean-difference direction did remove the linearly readable digit signal, as judged on tokens the probe never saw.

That result is not guaranteed. Run the same command with --label after_period and the refitted probe keeps a held-out correlation of 0.112 against 0.740 before: most of the signal gone, not all. And lesson 15 shows the opposite kind of case, where removing the least-squares direction itself leaves a refitted probe at 0.696. Removing one direction removes one direction. Whether a label stays readable from what is left is a question about the data, answered by refitting on held-out tokens, never by the identity that the removed direction now reads zero.

What removal preserves

Because the removed part and the remainder are orthogonal, ‖x‖² = (x · d̂)² + ‖x − (x · d̂) d̂‖². The energy accounting is exact: the squared norm lost from each token is exactly its squared coefficient along d. Summed over tokens and divided by n − 1, that is the variance along d, which is the quantity PCA ranks directions by in lesson 11. Removing the top principal component removes more squared norm than removing any other single direction; removing a probe direction removes however much variance happens to lie along it, which can be very little even when the direction predicts the label well.

In interpretability work

“We ablated the feature direction and the behaviour persisted” is interpretable only with the numbers above in hand: what fraction of the norm the direction carried, whether a refitted probe on held-out data still reads the label, and whether the direction removed was the one the behaviour used. A direction that reads a label is not thereby the direction the model computes with.

Pitfall

Removing several non-orthogonal directions one after another and assuming all of them are gone. Removing d₂ after d₁ reintroduces a component along d₁ if d₁ · d₂ ≠ 0. Orthonormalise the set with Gram–Schmidt first, then remove the whole subspace.

Check your understanding

After removing d, the mean-difference direction refitted on the same tokens is zero. A colleague concludes “the digit feature has been erased.” Which line of the output supports a weaker but defensible version, and what is it?

Reveal the reasoning

The fourth line: a least-squares probe refitted on the edited activations has held-out correlation −0.053. The defensible statement is “after removing d, no linear probe fitted on these 211 tokens reads the digit label on the other 90”, which is a claim about linear readability on this layer and sample. It says nothing about whether the model still computes with digit information through non-linear paths, through other layers, or through directions a bigger sample would reveal, and the after_period case shows a residual signal can survive.

Practice

Remove the after-period direction and read the refitted probe’s held-out correlation against the unedited one.

python3 labs/linalg_lab.py remove --label after_period
What to look for

Expect 0.112 after against 0.740 before. The mean-difference direction captured most but not all of what a probe can read; the remainder lives in directions the mean difference does not point along. Only the held-out refit can tell you that.
