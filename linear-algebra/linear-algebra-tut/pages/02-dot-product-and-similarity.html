Module 0 · Vectors

Dot product, norm, and cosine similarity

The dot product is the one operation that turns two vectors into a number. Length and angle are both built from it, and “similar activations” means one of them.

One operation, three readings

The dot product of u and v multiplies matching coordinates and adds the results: u · v = u₁v₁ + u₂v₂ + … + u_dv_d. For u = (3, 1) and v = (1, 2) that is 3 + 2 = 5. It is a single number. It is symmetric, u · v = v · u, and linear in each argument: (2u) · v = 2(u · v) and (u + w) · v = u · v + w · v. Those two properties are why it survives into 768 dimensions without changing character.

u · v = Σᵢ uᵢ vᵢ
‖u‖ = √(u · u) (the norm, or length)
cos θ = u · v / (‖u‖ ‖v‖) (cosine similarity, between −1 and 1)

The norm of u is the square root of u · u. For (3, 4) that is √(9 + 16) = 5, the Pythagorean length, and the formula is the same for 768 coordinates. The geometric fact that makes the dot product useful is u · v = ‖u‖ ‖v‖ cos θ, where θ is the angle between the arrows. So the dot product is large when the vectors are long and aligned, zero when they are perpendicular, negative when they point against each other. Dividing by both norms removes length and leaves the cosine of the angle: the cosine similarity. The 3Blue1Brown chapter on dot products shows the geometry; this lesson uses it.

Which one is “similarity”?

When someone says two activations are similar they might mean a large dot product or a large cosine. The two disagree whenever norms differ, and in this fixture norms differ by a factor of thirty-six. Ask the lab for the norms and for the tokens most similar to token 12.

python3 labs/linalg_lab.py similar

  token 274 '42'           cos 0.799  dot     6252.8  norm 88.4
  token  70 ' 1896'        cos 0.753  dot     6204.6  norm 93.0

  token 229 ' every'       cos 0.314  dot     2353.4  norm 84.6

cosine to every other token: mean 0.460, min 0.239, max 0.857

Three things to read. First, the tokens nearest to '42' by cosine are '71' (the second half of 1871), the other '42' later in the passage, and three year tokens. The activation geometry after block 6 groups number pieces together, which is what a “digit direction” will exploit in the next lesson. Second, the ranking by dot product is not the same as the ranking by cosine: ' 1903' has a larger dot product than ' 1896' but a smaller cosine, because its norm is larger. Third, no cosine is near zero. The mean cosine between token 12 and every other token is 0.460, and the least similar token, ' a', still has cosine 0.239. Activations in this layer all share a large common component, the mean you met in lesson 01, so raw cosines are compressed into a band well above zero. If you want “unrelated” to read as zero you must centre first.

The first token

Token 0 has norm 3,047, the median token 85. This is an observed property of the fixture (the README records it), and it is the first thing a norm computation would have told you about this data. It matters operationally: any dot product with token 0 is about thirty-six times larger than the same angle would produce with another token; any average over tokens that includes position 0 is dominated by it; and PCA on the raw matrix, in lesson 11, spends its first component almost entirely on this one row. The lab excludes position 0 from most commands and says so each time. The lesson is not “GPT-2 is odd”, it is “compute norms before you compute anything that sums over tokens”.

A worked two-dimensional check

u = (3, 4), v = (4, −3). Then u · v = 12 − 12 = 0, so they are perpendicular; cosine 0 without computing any norm. Now v = (6, 8) = 2u: u · v = 18 + 32 = 50, ‖u‖ = 5, ‖v‖ = 10, cosine 50/50 = 1. Same direction, different length, cosine exactly one. Finally v = (−3, −4): cosine −1. Three cases, three readings of the same formula; the browser projection explorer lets you drag u and v and watch the cosine move.

In interpretability work

Nearest-neighbour searches over activations, similarity between a probe direction and a neuron, and the attention score between a query and a key are all dot products or cosines. Decide, and state, which one you are using and whether you centred. A nearest-neighbour list computed on uncentred vectors with one giant row is a list of tokens that most resemble the giant row.

Pitfall

Reporting a cosine similarity of 0.46 as “moderate similarity”. In this layer 0.46 is the mean cosine between a number token and an arbitrary token. What counts as high is relative to that background, which you measure, not assume. Lesson 14 turns this into a baseline.

Check your understanding

Two directions d₁ and d₂ have cosine 0.9. Someone projects every activation onto each and reports that the two projections correlate at 0.9 across tokens. Is that a finding?

Reveal the reasoning

Not by itself. If d₁ and d₂ are nearly the same direction, the projections onto them are nearly the same numbers for every input, so a high correlation between them is guaranteed by the geometry of the directions and says nothing about the data. A finding would be two directions with low cosine whose projections nevertheless track each other, or one direction whose projection tracks an external label (lesson 03) on held-out tokens (lesson 14).

Practice

Query with token 0, the outlier. Read the dot products and the cosines.

python3 labs/linalg_lab.py similar --token 0
What to look for

The dot products are in the hundreds of thousands, because every one includes the factor 3,047. The cosines are still ordinary numbers between −1 and 1; dividing by the norms removed the scale. Whether the cosine ranking for token 0 means anything is another matter: a vector thirty-six times longer than its neighbours may be doing something other than representing the word “The”, and this course makes no claim about what.
