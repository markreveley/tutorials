Module 1 · Linear maps

Basis and change of basis

The 768 coordinates of an activation are its coefficients in one basis. Rewrite the same vector in another basis and every number changes while the vector does not. The neuron basis is one choice among infinitely many.

What a basis is

A basis of a d-dimensional space is a set of d vectors such that every vector in the space is exactly one linear combination of them. The coefficients of that combination are the vector’s coordinates in the basis. The standard basis of ℝ² is e₁ = (1, 0) and e₂ = (0, 1), and the coordinates of (3, 2) in it are, unsurprisingly, 3 and 2. But b₁ = (1, 0) and b₂ = (1, 1) are also a basis, and in it (3, 2) = 1·b₁ + 2·b₂ has coordinates (1, 2). Same arrow, different numbers. The 3Blue1Brown chapter on change of basis shows this with a skewed grid drawn over the usual one; the lab prints the same example.

v = c₁ b₁ + c₂ b₂ + … + c_d b_d (coordinates c in basis B)
B c = v, with the basis vectors as the columns of B; c = B⁻¹ v
if B is orthonormal: cᵢ = v · bᵢ, and ‖c‖ = ‖v‖

Finding coordinates means solving B c = v. For a general basis that is a linear solve. For an orthonormal basis, where the vectors are unit length and mutually perpendicular, it collapses to projection: each coordinate is the dot product with the corresponding basis vector, by lesson 03, and the norm of the coordinate list equals the norm of the vector, by Pythagoras applied d times. Rotations and reflections are exactly the changes of basis that preserve all dot products, which is why they are the ones that leave geometry alone.

The neuron basis is a choice

The 768 numbers in a residual-stream activation are its coordinates in the standard basis of ℝ⁷⁶⁸, which interpretability calls the neuron basis because coordinate i is what “neuron i” would read if one read the stream directly. The framework paper observes that this basis has no special status for the residual stream: “the residual stream doesn’t have a ‘privileged basis’; we could rotate it by rotating all the matrices interacting with it, without changing model behavior.” [Elhage et al. 2021] Rotate the stream by an orthonormal matrix R and replace every weight W that reads it by Rᵀ W and every weight that writes it by W R, and every computation the model performs is unchanged. The numbers in the activation file would all be different. So the question “what does coordinate 447 mean” has an answer only relative to the basis the weights happen to be stored in. It is not meaningless, since the stored basis is a real basis, but it is not privileged either, and nothing forces an interesting direction to line up with an axis.

The lab writes token 12 in two bases: the neuron basis, and the orthonormal basis of principal directions that lesson 11 will derive from the activation matrix.

python3 labs/linalg_lab.py basis
token 12 '42'
neuron-basis coordinates, first five: [-2.182 -2.564  1.455  1.724 -1.904]
PCA-basis coordinates, first five:    [ 17.301 -24.111  17.668 -23.132 -38.11 ]
same vector: B @ c reproduces x, max error 9.10e-14
norm in neuron basis 88.509; norm of PCA coordinates 88.509 (equal: the basis is orthonormal)
coordinate with largest magnitude: neuron basis index 447 (41.8); PCA basis index 4 (38.1)
a 2-D non-orthogonal basis with columns (1,0) and (1,1): coordinates of (3,2) are [1. 2.]

Every coordinate changed; the norm did not. In the neuron basis the largest coordinate is 41.8 at index 447, the coordinate lesson 00 asked you to keep in mind. In the PCA basis the largest coordinate is 38.1 at index 4, a direction that is a combination of all 768 neurons. Neither is more real than the other. Both are the same arrow measured against different rulers. The last line is the two-dimensional example done numerically: (3, 2) has coordinates (1, 2) in the skewed basis.

Why “a direction” need not be a neuron

Put the last two ideas together. A direction d is a vector; in the neuron basis it has 768 coordinates, and a neuron is the special case where all but one are zero. The digit direction of lesson 03 has all 768 coordinates nonzero. The principal directions of lesson 11 do too. If a feature were aligned with a neuron, projecting onto that neuron would read it; if it is not, projecting onto any single neuron reads a fraction of it mixed with fractions of whatever else has a component along that axis. This is the geometric content of the claim that reading individual neurons is a poor guide to the residual stream, and it is a statement about bases, not about any particular model.

The reverse caution also holds. Some activations in a transformer do have a privileged basis: the framework paper names tokens, attention patterns, and MLP activations, because an elementwise nonlinearity such as a ReLU or GELU acts coordinate by coordinate and so singles out the axes. [Elhage et al. 2021] Whether you may treat axis i as meaningful depends on which activation you are looking at.

In interpretability work

When a result is stated in terms of residual-stream neurons, ask what would happen to it under a rotation of the stream. If the answer is “it would look completely different”, the result is about the storage format. Probe directions, PCA directions and SAE directions are all attempts to find a basis that is better than the stored one for a particular purpose; which purpose decides which basis.

Pitfall

Reading coordinates in a non-orthonormal basis as projections. In the skewed basis above, (3, 2) has coordinate 1 along b₁ = (1, 0), but its projection onto b₁ is 3. Dot products give coordinates only when the basis is orthonormal; otherwise solve the system.

Check your understanding

A paper reports that “neuron 447 fires strongly for every token in layer 6”. Given lesson 00 and this lesson, what is the most defensible restatement?

Reveal the reasoning

That in the stored basis, coordinate 447 of the residual stream after block 6 is large for every token in this sample, with mean 45.8 and the largest magnitude of any coordinate; and that because the residual stream has no privileged basis, this describes a large component of the mean activation along one axis of the storage basis rather than a “neuron” computing something. Whether a rotation-invariant statement holds, such as “the mean activation has a large norm concentrated in a low-dimensional subspace”, can be checked with the covariance and PCA of lessons 10 and 11.

Practice

Write token 29 ('71', a number token similar to token 12 by lesson 02) in both bases and compare its PCA-basis coordinates with token 12’s.

python3 labs/linalg_lab.py basis --token 29
What to look for

The norms agree between bases again. If the two number tokens are similar as vectors, their coordinates in the PCA basis should be similar coordinate by coordinate, as should their neuron-basis coordinates; similarity does not depend on basis when the basis is orthonormal. What differs is readability: the first PCA coordinates are large and few, the neuron coordinates small and many.
