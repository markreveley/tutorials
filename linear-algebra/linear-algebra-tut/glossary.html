Reference

Glossary

Definitions in the language of activations and directions. Linked terms in lessons also show keyboard-accessible tooltips.

Activation

The vector a model component outputs for one token at one layer; here a row of the residual stream after block 6 of GPT-2 small, length 768.

Attention head weights

For one head, the matrices W_Q, W_K, W_V of shape (d_model, d_head) and W_O of shape (d_head, d_model); the products W_Q W_Kᵀ and W_V W_O have rank at most d_head.

Baseline

A comparison that would also score well if the claim were empty, such as a random direction or shuffled labels.

Basis

A set of vectors such that every vector in the space is exactly one linear combination of them; coordinates are the coefficients.

Centering

Subtracting the mean row from every row, so that the data have mean zero before PCA or covariance.

Change of basis

Rewriting the same vector in the coordinates of a different basis; for an orthonormal basis the new coordinates are dot products.

Correlation

The cosine similarity of two centred sequences; between −1 and 1, zero when unrelated in the linear sense.

Cosine similarity

The dot product of two vectors divided by the product of their norms; the cosine of the angle between them, between −1 and 1.

Covariance matrix

The (d, d) matrix of covariances between pairs of coordinates across the rows; its diagonal holds variances.

Direction

A unit vector, or any nonzero vector read only for its orientation; a feature direction is a candidate axis along which activations are measured.

Dot product

The sum of the products of matching coordinates of two vectors; a scalar that grows with alignment and with length.

Eigenvalue

The scale factor λ attached to an eigenvector.

Eigenvector

A nonzero vector that a square matrix only scales: A v = λ v.

einsum

A notation naming each axis by a letter, so that repeated letters are summed over; makes the shape of a product explicit.

Gradient

The vector of partial derivatives of a scalar function; the direction of steepest increase.

Gradient ascent

Repeatedly stepping along the gradient to increase a scalar function.

Gram–Schmidt

A procedure that turns a list of vectors into an orthonormal list with the same span, by subtracting projections in order.

Held-out

Data not used to fit a direction, kept for checking what the direction predicts.

Identity matrix

The square matrix I with ones on the diagonal; the map that changes nothing.

Least squares

Choosing coefficients to minimise the sum of squared differences between predictions and targets.

Linear combination

A sum of vectors each multiplied by a scalar, such as 2u − 3v.

Linear independence

No vector in the set is a linear combination of the others; equivalently the only combination giving zero has all-zero coefficients.

Linear map

A function that respects addition and scaling: f(u + v) = f(u) + f(v) and f(cu) = c f(u). Every matrix defines one and every linear map between coordinate spaces is a matrix.

Linear probe

A direction and offset fitted to predict a label from activations; its projection is the probe score.

Low-rank approximation

Keeping only the k largest singular triples; the best rank-k approximation in the least-squares sense.

Matrix

A rectangular grid of numbers with a shape (rows, columns); read as a linear map, a batch of row vectors, or a table.

Matrix multiplication

Combining an (n, k) and a (k, m) matrix into an (n, m) matrix whose entries are dot products of rows with columns; composition of linear maps.

Neuron basis

The standard basis in which coordinate i is the i-th entry of the activation; one basis among many, with no special status in the residual stream.

Norm

The length of a vector: the square root of its dot product with itself.

Normal equations

XᵀX w = Xᵀy: the condition that the least-squares residual is orthogonal to every column of X.

Orthogonal

Two vectors with dot product zero; at right angles.

Orthonormal

A set of unit vectors that are pairwise orthogonal.

Outer product

The (n, m) matrix u vᵀ formed from an n-vector and an m-vector; every rank-one matrix is one.

PCA

Principal component analysis: the orthonormal directions of greatest variance in a centred data matrix, which are the eigenvectors of its covariance.

Permutation test

Recomputing a statistic many times with the labels shuffled, to see what values arise when there is no relation.

Principal component

One of the PCA directions, ordered by variance explained.

Projection

The component of a vector along a direction; its coefficient is the dot product with the unit direction, a scalar per token.

Rank

The number of linearly independent columns (equivalently rows) of a matrix; the dimension of the space it maps onto.

Residual

What is left of a vector after its projection is subtracted; orthogonal to the direction.

Residual stream

The running vector each transformer layer reads from and adds to; the sum of the embedding and every earlier layer output.

Ridge

Least squares with a penalty on the squared norm of the coefficients; shrinks the solution and makes it unique when there are more columns than rows.

Scalar

A single number, as opposed to a vector or matrix.

Scree plot

Variance explained plotted against component number, used to see how quickly the components stop mattering.

Shape

The tuple of array dimensions, such as (302, 768); the type signature of a tensor.

Singular value

A diagonal entry of Σ: how much the matrix stretches along the matching right singular vector.

Span

All linear combinations of a set of vectors; a line, plane, or higher flat through the origin.

Subspace

A flat through the origin closed under addition and scaling; the span of some vectors.

Superposition

A representation holding more features than it has dimensions, with feature directions that are not all orthogonal; defined in Toy Models of Superposition.

SVD

Singular value decomposition: A = U Σ Vᵀ with orthonormal U and V and non-negative singular values in Σ, for any matrix.

Symmetric matrix

A square matrix equal to its transpose; its eigenvectors can be chosen orthonormal and its eigenvalues are real.

Transpose

The matrix with rows and columns swapped, written Aᵀ.

Unit vector

A vector of norm 1; any nonzero vector divided by its norm.

Variance

The average squared deviation from the mean; along a unit direction, the variance of the projection coefficients.

Variance explained

The fraction of total variance lying along a component; the component’s eigenvalue divided by the sum of all eigenvalues.

Vector

An ordered list of numbers of fixed length, read as a point or an arrow in a space with that many dimensions. An activation is a vector.
