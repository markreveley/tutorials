Module 2 · Arrays and tensors

Broadcasting rules, with a shape-prediction drill

Broadcasting is one rule, applied from the right. Learn it as a procedure you run by hand and most shape errors become predictable.

The rule, as a procedure

When two arrays meet in an operation, NumPy compares their shapes element-wise starting with the trailing dimension and working left. Two dimensions are compatible when they are equal or one of them is 1; otherwise the operation raises ValueError: operands could not be broadcast together [NumPy: broadcasting]. Torch states the same rule and adds the case explicitly: a dimension may also be missing, in which case 1 is prepended to the shorter shape until the lengths match, and each result dimension is the larger of the two [PyTorch: broadcasting].

a: (2, 5, 768) b: (768,) c: (2, 1, 768) align right: (1, 1, 768) (2, 1, 768) compare: 2 vs 1 ok → 2 5 vs 1 ok → 5 768 vs 768 ok → 768 result: (2, 5, 768) (2, 5, 768)

Run it by hand: write the shapes right-aligned, pad the shorter with ones on the left, and go column by column. A size-1 axis is stretched, conceptually, to match; no memory is copied. Any column with two different sizes neither of which is 1 is an error, and the error message names both shapes, so read it.

mean over d without keepdims: (2, 3)
acts - mean fails: operands could not be broadcast together
with keepdims: (2, 3, 1) -> (acts - mean_k).shape = (2, 3, 4)
explicit alternative mean[..., None]: (2, 3, 1)

Read the drill

(4, 3) + (3,): a row added to every row. (4, 3) + (4, 1): a column added to every column. (4, 3) + (4,) fails, and this is the one people expect to work: the 4 aligns with the 3, not with the 4, because alignment is from the right. To add a per-row value you need (4, 1), which is what v[:, None] or keepdims gives you. (8, 1, 6, 1) + (7, 1, 5) is the textbook case where every axis stretches somewhere. (12, 5, 5) + (5, 5) adds one mask to every head. (2, 5, 768) + (5,) fails because 5 meets 768.

The reduction that breaks the broadcast

The second half of the output is the mistake you will make most. acts.mean(axis=-1) drops the last axis, giving (2, 3), and acts - mean aligns 3 against 4 and fails. With keepdims=True the mean is (2, 3, 1), which broadcasts back against (2, 3, 4) exactly as intended. mean[..., None] does the same by hand. Centering, normalising, and standardising activations are all this pattern, and the row_normalize and center_last_axis stubs in the shape lab are its two most common instances.

Make the intent explicit

Broadcasting is silent, which is the danger. (5, 768) + (768,) adds a bias to every token; (5, 768) + (5, 1) adds a per-token scalar; both run. If you meant the other one you get a number, not an error. Three habits help. Write the shape comment next to each operand. Insert axes with None rather than relying on the implicit left-padding, so the alignment you intend is the one on the page. And when a result is a shape you did not predict, stop: a (5, 5) where you wanted (5,) is almost always a broadcast that produced an outer product.

In-place operations have one extra restriction. The in-place tensor cannot change shape as a result of the broadcast [PyTorch: broadcasting], so a += b works when b broadcasts to a's shape and fails when the result would be larger than a.

In eval and interpretability work

Subtracting a mean activation from a cache, projecting onto a direction, and applying an attention mask to every head are all broadcasts. The linear-algebra course will make you do the first two with real GPT-2 activations; the shape rule is the same there.

Pitfall

Averaging over a padded batch. acts.mean(axis=1) on a (batch, seq, d) cache includes the padding positions. masked_mean_pool in the lab exists so you have written the correct version once: multiply by the mask with a trailing axis added, sum, and divide by the mask's count.

Check your understanding

You compute scores = q @ k.T with q of shape (5, 64) and k of shape (5, 64), then scores - scores.max(axis=-1) before a softmax. The result has shape (5, 5) and no error. Is it right?

Reveal the reasoning

No. scores.max(axis=-1) is (5,), which aligns against the last axis of (5, 5): each column gets a different row's maximum subtracted. The softmax then has the wrong stabiliser per row and, worse, a result that is subtly wrong rather than an error. keepdims=True gives (5, 1) and subtracts each row's own maximum.

Practice

Implement broadcast_shape, row_normalize, center_last_axis and masked_mean_pool in labs/shapes.py. Run only the shape tests. Before each run, say which tests you expect to pass.

What to look for

The random-shape test will catch an off-by-one in your alignment that the hand-written cases miss. For row_normalize, the zero-row test forces you to divide only where the norm is nonzero; np.divide(..., out=..., where=...) or a masked assignment both work.
