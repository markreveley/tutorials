## Exercise

1. Write `labs/m1-18/tree-harness.py`. Launch the supplied `tree --seconds 10` fixture with `subprocess.Popen`, `start_new_session=True`, and captured stdout/stderr. Read its first JSON line to obtain the child PID; retain the Popen handle for the direct parent. Record both live processes and their process-group/session IDs before signaling anything.

2. In a `direct` case, terminate only that parent after observing both processes. Wait for the parent and separately time when the captured output reaches EOF (end of file). Bound `communicate` with a timeout longer than the fixture's 10 seconds and retain a timeout as an incomplete observation. Predict the two completion times before running.

3. In a fresh `group` case, obtain the new parent's group ID with `os.getpgid(parent.pid)`. Verify it is positive, belongs to this still-running child, and differs from the harness's own group before calling `os.killpg` with SIGTERM. Capture fresh process observations and reader completion. Never use 0, an arbitrary negative PID, or an ID copied from a previous run.

4. Run `timeout --kill-after=2s 20s python3 labs/m1-18/tree-harness.py direct`, then repeat with `group`. Implement these two fixed case names only. Record the commands and comparison in `labs/m1-18/cleanup-comparison.md`; handle missing processes and timeout errors explicitly. Explain why a cooperating group is useful for cleanup yet cannot prevent hostile descendants from changing groups. Keep this harness separate from the runner.

Keep commands and actual results in the named evidence file; use the attempt's **Work and observations** for help received or a pointer to that file, not a duplicate transcript. Under **Learner explanation**, briefly compare your result with your prediction and identify remaining uncertainty. Implementation help during the exercise is allowed and must be recorded.
