Attention II: heads as a QK circuit and an OV circuit

Twelve heads in parallel, each a pair of low-rank maps: one decides where to look, the other decides what to move. A real GPT-2 pattern shows what a pattern is and is not.

Twelve heads, one matrix

GPT-2 computes all heads of a layer with one matrix. c_attn.weight has shape (768, 2304): the normalised stream times this matrix gives a 2304-vector that is split into query, key, and value blocks of 768 each, and each 768-block is split into twelve head slices of 64. Head h's W_Q is columns 64h to 64h+63 of the query block; likewise for keys and values. After each head produces its (seq, 64) output, the twelve outputs are concatenated into (seq, 768) and multiplied by c_proj.weight, shape (768, 768). Vaswani et al. define multi-head attention exactly this way, Concat(head1…headh)W^O [Vaswani 2017].

Concatenate-then-multiply equals sum-of-per-head-products: rows 64h to 64h+63 of c_proj.weight are head h's own output matrix W_O, shape (64, 768), and the layer's output is Σh (head h output)·W_Oh plus one shared bias. Elhage et al. make this the organising fact: heads are independent and additive, each writing its own vector into the stream [Elhage 2021].

Two circuits

Follow one head from stream to stream. The score between a query residual xi and a key residual xj is (xiW_Q)·(xjW_K) = xi (W_Q W_Kᵀ) xjᵀ. The matrix W_Q W_Kᵀ is 768 × 768 but has rank at most 64, because it is a product through a 64-wide bottleneck. Elhage et al. call this the QK circuit: it alone determines the attention pattern [Elhage 2021]. (They write it as W_QᵀW_K with column-vector conventions; this course uses row vectors, so the transpose moves.)

Separately, what the head writes for an attended position j is xj W_V W_O, weighted by the pattern. The matrix W_V W_O, also 768 × 768 and rank at most 64, is the OV circuit: it alone determines what information moves, given the pattern. The two circuits share nothing but the input, so you can analyse "where does this head look?" and "what does it do with what it finds?" as separate questions, each about one low-rank matrix.

patternij = softmaxj( xi (W_Q W_Kᵀ) xjᵀ / √d_head )
head outputi = Σj patternij · xj (W_V W_O)

Every position puts all of its weight on position 0, the position whose residual norm was 3,000 in lesson 03. On this prompt, this head's output at every position is the same vector, x0W_V W_O. Whether that is "the head doing nothing" or "the head writing a constant" is a question about the OV circuit and the downstream reader, not about the pattern; the pattern only says the head is not moving information between content tokens here.

What a pattern is and is not

A pattern is a function of two things: the QK circuit and the actual residual vectors at the query and key positions on this prompt. Change the prompt and the pattern changes, even though the circuit is fixed. So a pattern is evidence about a head's behaviour on one input, and the QK matrix is the object that generalises. A pattern says nothing about what is written: two heads with identical patterns and different OV circuits do completely different things. And a pattern entry of 1.00 is not "strong"; the softmax forces every row to sum to 1, so a position that attends to nothing in particular still has to put its weight somewhere.

In interpretability work

State a head claim in two halves and test each. QK half: "on prompts of family F, the pattern at position i puts more than p of its weight on the position with property P," tested on held-out prompts with a shuffled-prompt control. OV half: "the vector xjW_V W_O increases the logit of token T," tested by projecting onto the unembedding, with random-head and random-token controls. A head that passes the QK test and fails the OV test is attending without doing the thing claimed.

Pitfall

Computing the OV circuit as W_O W_V or the QK circuit as W_K W_Qᵀ because a paper wrote it that way. With row-vector weights of shape (768, 64), the products that give 768 × 768 matrices are W_Q W_Kᵀ and W_V W_O; the other orders give 64 × 64 matrices or shape errors. The lab test checks that xq·QK·xk equals (xqW_Q)·(xkW_K).

Check your understanding

Two heads in different layers have the same QK circuit (identical W_Q W_K^T) but one has its OV circuit scaled by zero. On a given prompt, do they produce the same pattern? The same output? What would an ablation of each head show?

Reveal the reasoning

Same pattern: the pattern depends only on the QK circuit and the residual inputs, and both heads read the same prompt (if they are in different layers their inputs differ, so even the patterns could differ; assume the same layer for the clean case). Different output: the zero-OV head writes nothing. Ablating it changes nothing downstream; ablating the other changes whatever read its write. The pattern alone cannot distinguish them, which is why pattern-only evidence is weak.

What to look for

The bias toward the first token persists across prompts; the exact weights do not. split_heads must move the head axis to position 1 so that (batch, seq, d_model) becomes (batch, n_head, seq, d_head); the test checks that head 1 owns columns 2–3 of an 8-wide input. The circuit test asserts rank at most d_head and the bilinear identity.
