Module 2 · MLPs and the output

Gated MLPs: SwiGLU in Llama and Qwen3

Newer models replace the two-matrix MLP with three matrices and an elementwise product. The formula is short, the pencil example is shorter, and the change matters for what "a neuron" means.

The formula

Shazeer's 2020 note proposed replacing the first matrix-and-activation of the feed-forward layer with a gated linear unit. Two parallel projections of the input are combined by an elementwise product, and one of them first passes through a nonlinearity. With the Swish nonlinearity, Swishβ(x) = x·σ(βx), and β = 1, the variant is SwiGLU, and the whole layer is [Shazeer 2020]:

FFNSwiGLU(x, W, V, W2) = (Swish1(xW) ⊙ xV) W2

Three matrices instead of two. The paper keeps parameter count constant by shrinking the hidden width by a factor of 2/3 relative to the ungated version, and reports that SwiGLU and GEGLU gave its best pre-training perplexities on T5-style models [Shazeer 2020]. Swish1 is the same function PyTorch calls SiLU.

A two-dimensional pencil example

Take x = [1, 2] and 2 × 2 matrices chosen so the arithmetic is easy. Let xW = [0, 2] and xV = [3, 1]. Swish1(0) = 0·σ(0) = 0 and Swish1(2) = 2·σ(2) = 2 × 0.8808 = 1.7616, using σ(2) = 1/(1 + e−2) = 0.8808. The elementwise product with [3, 1] is [0, 1.7616]: the first coordinate is gated off entirely even though its "up" value is 3, the largest number in the example. Summing through W2 = [1, 1]ᵀ gives 1.7616.

python labs/anatomy.py swiglu-demo
x = [1.0, 2.0]; xW (gate) = [0.0, 2.0]; xV (up) = [3.0, 1.0]
Swish_1(xW) = xW * sigmoid(xW) = [0.0000, 1.7616]   (sigmoid(2) = 0.8808)
Swish_1(xW) * xV (elementwise) = [0.0000, 1.7616]
FFN_SwiGLU(x) = (Swish_1(xW) * xV) W2 = [1.7616]
torch.nn.functional.silu agrees with Swish_1: [0.0000, 1.7616]

That gating-off is the point. In GPT-2's MLP a hidden unit's activation is a single nonlinear function of one dot product. In SwiGLU, each hidden unit is the product of two different dot products of the input, one squashed by Swish and one not. The gate can be zero while the up-projection is large, or the up-projection can be negative and flip the sign of a positive gate.

Which models, verified

Two sources settle which models use this. The LLaMA paper states that it replaces ReLU with SwiGLU, with a hidden dimension of 2/3 · 4d rather than 4d [Touvron 2023]. The Qwen3 technical report lists SwiGLU among the components of its dense models, alongside grouped-query attention, rotary embeddings, and RMSNorm [Qwen3 report]. The installed Hugging Face code confirms the formula for both: LlamaMLP and Qwen3MLP each compute down_proj(act_fn(gate_proj(x)) * up_proj(x)) with hidden_act = "silu" and no biases [transformers Llama and Qwen3 code]. The naming map is therefore:

ShazeerHugging Face Llama / Qwen3Shape (hidden d, inner f)
W (gate)gate_proj.weight(f, d) as nn.Linear; applied as x Wᵀ
V (up)up_proj.weight(f, d)
W₂ (down)down_proj.weight(d, f)
Swish₁act_fn = SiLUelementwise

Note the orientation difference from GPT-2: these are nn.Linear layers storing (out, in), while GPT-2's Conv1D stores (in, out). A loader that assumes one convention will silently transpose the other.

What "a neuron" becomes

In a gated MLP, hidden unit n still has an output direction, row n of W2, and still has a scalar activation, the product Swish(xW)n · (xV)n, so neuron-level activation analysis carries over unchanged: hook the product, not either factor alone. But it now has two input directions, column n of W and column n of V, and the activation is not a monotone function of either. An explanation of a SwiGLU neuron is a claim about a bilinear-ish function of the input, and the "which direction does this neuron read?" question has two answers.

In interpretability work

Before porting a GPT-2 neuron method to a Llama-family or Qwen model, check three things against the installed model code: the activation is the gated product (hook mlp.act_fn output times up_proj output, or the input to down_proj), the inner width (2/3 · 4d in LLaMA, read from intermediate_size in the config), and the absence of biases. TransformerLens's config for such models sets gated_mlp; its mlp.hook_post is the value fed to the down-projection.

Pitfall

Assuming the "gate" is the branch that passes through the nonlinearity in every implementation. In the Shazeer formula and the Hugging Face code it is: act_fn(gate_proj(x)) * up_proj(x). Other codebases name the branches differently. Read the forward method, not the parameter names.

Check your understanding

In the pencil example, keep xW = [0, 2] and change xV to [3, −1]. What is the output, and what does the sign flip tell you about reading the gate alone?

Reveal the reasoning

Swish(xW) is unchanged, [0, 1.7616]; the product becomes [0, −1.7616]; summing through W₂ = [1, 1]ᵀ gives −1.7616. A positive gate value does not mean the unit writes in its "positive" direction; the up-projection sets the sign and magnitude, the gate sets how much passes. Hooking only the post-Swish gate would show the same value for an output of +1.7616 and −1.7616.

Practice

Recompute sigmoid(2) with a calculator (1/(1 + e^−2)) and the final 1.7616. Then read the LlamaMLP and Qwen3MLP forward methods in your installed transformers package and confirm the order of operations against the table.

What to look for

e^−2 = 0.1353, 1/(1.1353) = 0.8808, 2 × 0.8808 = 1.7616. In the installed code both forward methods are the single line down_proj(act_fn(gate_proj(x)) * up_proj(x)); the activation is silu because the config's hidden_act is "silu".
