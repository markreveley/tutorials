Module 0 · The object of study

The residual stream: a sum of writes

Every block adds to the stream; nothing overwrites it. That one algebraic fact is what makes attribution, direction probing, and circuit tracing possible.

The stream is a sum

Write x0 for the embedded input of one position. Block i computes an attention output ai from the stream and adds it, then computes an MLP output mi from the updated stream and adds that:

xmid,i = xi + ai(xi)
xi+1 = xmid,i + mi(xmid,i)

Unrolling twelve blocks gives the final stream as a plain sum: x12 = x0 + Σi ai + Σi mi. Each term is a 768-vector that one component wrote. The components are nonlinear in what they read, but the stream itself is linear in what they wrote. Elhage et al. call this the residual stream being a communication channel and note two consequences this course relies on: the stream has no privileged basis (rotate every weight matrix that touches it and the model is unchanged), and any two layers are connected by "virtual weights," the product of one layer's output weights with a later layer's input weights [Elhage 2021].

The identity is checkable on the real model. The stream command uses the course's own forward pass to record every write at one position and adds them back up:

python labs/anatomy.py stream

    4        74.11        9.08      17.48         81.91

   10       165.14       28.63      79.21        237.87

max |embed + pos + sum(attn_out) + sum(mlp_out) - resid_post.11| = 0.00e+00

Read the first row. The stream enters block 0 with norm 5.4 (the embedding sum from lesson 02), attention adds a vector of norm 33.5, the MLP adds one of norm 36.7, and the stream leaves with norm 61.0. Norms do not add because the vectors point in different directions; 61 is less than 5.4 + 33.5 + 36.7 = 75.6. The last line is the identity: the embedding plus all twenty-four writes reproduces the final stream to floating-point exactness.

The stream grows, and position 0 is special

At position 5 the norm grows from 5 to 476 over twelve blocks, with the biggest single write coming from attention in block 11. Now look at position 0:

The MLP in block 2 writes a vector of norm 2,319 at position 0, and the stream there sits above 3,000 for most of the depth before block 11's attention write of norm 2,655 brings it back down to 379. This is an observation about this checkpoint on this prompt, printed from the weights; the course makes no claim about why. It is shown because it is the kind of thing you must notice before averaging anything over positions: a mean over positions that includes position 0 is dominated by it, and a cosine similarity between position 0 and anything else is close to meaningless. Lesson 06 shows an attention head that puts all of its weight on position 0.

Shapes

Every quantity in the table is a slice of a (batch, seq, d_model) tensor. resid_pre.i is the stream entering block i; resid_mid.i is after the attention write; resid_post.i is after the MLP write and equals resid_pre.i+1. These names are TransformerLens's, and Hugging Face's hidden_states tuple holds the same resid_pre values with an offset of one: hidden_states[i] is the stream entering block i, and hidden_states[12] is not resid_post.11 but the stream after the final layer norm. Lesson 13 verifies every one of these correspondences numerically.

In interpretability work

Because the stream is a sum, the question "which component made the model predict X?" has a well-defined first answer: project each write onto the direction that increases the logit of X, and the projections add up to the logit (after the final layer norm, which lesson 04 addresses). That is direct logit attribution. The same additivity lets you ablate one write (replace it with zero or with its mean) and measure what changes, which is the basic experiment of the interpretability course.

Pitfall

Reading resid_post.i as "what block i computed." It is the running total. What block i computed is attn_out.i and mlp_out.i, and the difference resid_post.i − resid_pre.i is their sum. Hugging Face's hidden_states only gives you the totals, so you must subtract adjacent entries, or hook the sub-modules, to recover the writes.

Check your understanding

The norm of resid_post.11 at position 5 is 476, and attention in block 11 wrote a vector of norm 215. Can you conclude that block 11 attention contributed about 45% of the final stream?

Reveal the reasoning

No. Norms of vectors in different directions do not add, and a fraction of the norm is not a fraction of anything the model uses. The meaningful decomposition is along a chosen direction: project each write onto, say, the unembedding direction of the predicted token and compare those scalars, which do add up. The write could be large in norm and nearly orthogonal to every direction that matters for the prediction.

Practice

Run stream at position 1 of the running prompt and at the last position of a different prompt. Compare the growth pattern with the two tables above.

python labs/anatomy.py stream "The cat sat on the mat" --position 1
python labs/anatomy.py stream "A dog lay on the rug" --position 5
What to look for

Position 1 grows steadily like position 5, without the position-0 spike; both prompts end with a large attention write in block 11. The exact numbers differ because the vectors differ; the identity line is 0.00e+00 in every case because the stream really is the sum of its writes.
