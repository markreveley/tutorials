Module 2 · MLPs and the output

The MLP block: two matrices, a GELU, and where a neuron lives

Each block's MLP is the same small network applied at every position. Its hidden units are the "neurons" of neuron-level interpretability, and they have a definite address.

Up, nonlinearity, down

The MLP reads the normalised residual vector at one position, projects it up to width 3,072 with c_fc, applies an elementwise nonlinearity, and projects back down to 768 with c_proj. Every position is processed independently with the same weights; the MLP never looks at other positions. The original Transformer's version used ReLU, FFN(x) = max(0, xW₁ + b₁)W₂ + b₂, with an inner width of 2,048 for d_model 512, the same 4× ratio GPT-2 keeps [Vaswani 2017].

pre = x W_in + b_in (768 → 3072)
post = GELU(pre) (elementwise)
out = post W_out + b_out (3072 → 768)

In Hugging Face's naming, W_in is mlp.c_fc.weight, shape (768, 3072), and W_out is mlp.c_proj.weight, shape (3072, 768). Both are Conv1D layers, which despite the name compute x @ weight + bias with the weight stored as (in, out), the transpose of nn.Linear's convention [transformers GPT-2 code]. The capstone has to respect that orientation.

GELU and GPT-2's approximation

GELU is x·Φ(x), where Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution function: the input scaled by the probability that a standard normal is below it. For large positive x it is nearly x; for large negative x it is nearly 0; in between it dips slightly below zero. Hendrycks and Gimpel give a tanh approximation, 0.5x(1 + tanh[√(2/π)(x + 0.044715x³)]) [Hendrycks & Gimpel 2016], and that approximation, not the exact function, is what GPT-2's config names as gelu_new and what the installed code computes [transformers GPT-2 code]. At x = 1, the exact value is Φ(1) = 0.8413 and the approximation gives 0.8412:

The difference between exact and approximate GELU is in the fourth decimal place at these inputs. It still matters for the capstone: a from-scratch implementation using torch.nn.functional.gelu with its default exact mode will be off from Hugging Face by small amounts that compound through twelve layers. Use approximate='tanh' or write the formula.

Where a neuron lives

Neuron n of a block's MLP is three things with one index. Its input direction is column n of W_in, a 768-vector: the neuron's pre-activation is the dot product of the normalised residual with this column, plus bias n. Its activation is the scalar GELU(pren) at each position, the number a "neuron fires on X" claim is about. Its output direction is row n of W_out, another 768-vector: the neuron's contribution to the stream is its activation times this row. The MLP's whole write is the sum over neurons, Σn postn · W_out[n].

So "neuron 1612 in layer 0" names column 1612 of h.0.mlp.c_fc.weight, entry 1612 of the post-GELU tensor (TransformerLens: blocks.0.mlp.hook_post[..., 1612]), and row 1612 of h.0.mlp.c_proj.weight. The demo shows that neuron 1612 fires at 11.49 on "The" at position 0 and does not appear in the top three anywhere else; neuron 2845 appears in the top three at four positions. These are observations on one prompt, not characterisations.

Most neurons are off

At each position only 7–24% of the 3,072 activations are positive. GELU is not exactly zero for negative inputs, but it is small, so most of the MLP's write at a position comes from a few hundred neurons. This is what makes neuron-level analysis tractable and also what makes it treacherous: a neuron with a large activation on your prompt may be one of thousands that fire on something, and the top-3 list changes from prompt to prompt.

A privileged basis

Unlike the residual stream, the MLP hidden layer has a privileged basis. The nonlinearity acts coordinate by coordinate, so rotating the hidden space would change the function. That is the reason neurons are candidate units of analysis at all: the 3,072 coordinates are singled out by the computation, while the 768 residual coordinates are not [Elhage 2021]. It does not follow that each neuron means one thing; a single neuron can respond to several unrelated inputs, and a feature can be spread across many neurons.

In interpretability work

A neuron explanation is a prediction about hook_post[..., n] on inputs you have not looked at. Write it as a scoring rule before looking: "on held-out text, activation above 2 occurs on tokens with property P and not otherwise." The control is a random neuron in the same layer scored by the same rule, and a version of the rule with shuffled labels. An explanation that cannot beat those has not been demonstrated; the evals course's module on evaluating explanations formalises this.

Pitfall

Reading off "top activating neurons" from one prompt and treating them as that prompt's "circuit." Top-k on one input selects for large magnitude, which correlates with how much variance a neuron has overall, not with relevance to the prediction. Compare against the neuron's activation distribution over a corpus, and against its contribution to the specific logit you care about.

Check your understanding

Neuron n has a large positive activation at the last position, but its output row W_out[n] is nearly orthogonal to the unembedding direction of the predicted token. What does the neuron contribute to that prediction, and what might it be doing instead?

Reveal the reasoning

Almost nothing directly: its write is activation × W_out[n], and projecting that onto the predicted token's unembedding direction gives a small logit contribution. It may be writing a direction read by later blocks (an intermediate feature), or contributing to other tokens' logits, or its write may matter only after ln_f rescales the stream. A large activation establishes that the neuron is responding to something, not that it is responsible for the output.

Practice

Run the MLP demo on layer 5 and on a different prompt at layer 0. Note which neuron indices recur. Then implement gelu_new and mlp in labs/gpt2_from_scratch.py and run the component tests.

python labs/anatomy.py mlp-demo --layer 5
python labs/anatomy.py mlp-demo "The dog sat on the rug"
What to look for

Some indices recur across prompts at layer 0 for the same tokens (" the" and " on" in particular), since layer-0 MLP activations depend mostly on the token identity and position; at layer 5 they depend on context and recur less. gelu_new(1) must equal 0.8412 to four places; the test also compares to torch's tanh-approximate GELU on five inputs.
