Rung 8 · the metal

GC & Process Memory

Rung 7 said each process has its own private heap. Now: how is that heap laid out, what happens when it fills, and why does the BEAM stay responsive under millions of processes? The answer is a collector that's the opposite of "stop the world."

A process is four blocks — and the stack meets the heap

"A process is basically four blocks of memory: a stack, a heap, a message area, and the Process Control Block (the PCB)" Ch.3.2. The clever part: the stack and heap share one allocated block and grow toward each other — the stack downward from the top, the heap upward from the bottom Ch.3. When the two pointers (stop and htop) meet, the process is out of room and the garbage collector runs.

one block
stack → (grows down/right) ← heap (grows up/left)
free gapthe white space in the middle — when it hits zero, GC runs

A copying collector: keep the living, abandon the dead

The BEAM uses a per-process copying generational collector Ch.3. A collection works like this: allocate a fresh area (the to-space), walk the stack to find the live roots, follow each root and copy the live data into the new heap, then free the entire old area at once. Garbage is never touched — it's reclaimed simply by not being copied. Copying also compacts: survivors end up packed together with no holes.

a copying, generational collection
young heap (from-space) — live / garbage
old heap — survivors of a previous collection (only swept on a full sweep)
Simplified: real collections scan stack roots and follow pointers. "Minor" = young only; promotion happens across the high_water mark; the old heap is collected only on a major/full sweep (Ch.3).

Generational: look at the young, mostly leave the old alone

Most garbage is short-lived, so scanning the whole heap every time wastes work. The collector is generational: a minor collection looks only at recent data; anything that has already survived (below the high_water mark) is treated as old and moved to a separate old heap, which is only collected during an occasional major collection / full sweep Ch.3. The fields driving this — high_water, old_heap, gen_gcs, max_gen_gcs — live in the PCB.

Why this is the BEAM's superpower Because heaps are private, a collection pauses only that one process — never a global stop-the-world across the millions of others. Combined with copying (cost scales with the live set, not the garbage) and generations (usually only a small young heap), you get the low, predictable latency the BEAM is famous for. The isolation that forced message-copying (next rung) is the very same isolation that makes GC cheap.
