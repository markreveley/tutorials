$ python3 labs/stats_lab.py quiz
Eval statistics: six judgment checks. Type 1, 2, or 3; q to quit. No data is saved.

1. A and B answered the same 200 tasks. What do you analyze?
  1. The 200 B-A task differences
  2. Two unrelated headline percentages
  3. Only the tasks B passed
Your answer: Correct. Keep the pairs: shared task difficulty can cancel. Choose an independent unit before computing uncertainty.

2. Ten tasks each ran 100 times. How many distinct tasks were sampled?
  1. 1000
  2. 10
  3. 100
Your answer: Correct. There are 10 tasks and 1000 attempts. Repeats inform within-task behavior; they do not add task coverage.

3. A 95% difference interval is [-2,+6] percentage points. What follows?
  1. The systems are equivalent
  2. B is better with 95% probability
  3. Both modest harm and useful benefit remain compatible
Your answer: Correct. Failure to reject zero is not equivalence. Compare the interval with a predeclared practical margin.

4. A grader has 98% accuracy; failures occur on 2% of cases. Next step?
  1. Deploy it
  2. Inspect failure recall and false alarms
  3. Round accuracy to 100%
Your answer: Correct. An always-pass grader already gets 98% accuracy. Inspect a confusion matrix against independently adjudicated labels.

5. You keep rerunning until p<.05. What changed?
  1. Only convenience
  2. The error properties of the decision rule
  3. The model became better
Your answer: Correct. Ordinary fixed-sample p-values do not justify this stopping rule. Plan the endpoint or use an appropriate sequential design.

6. You saw zero critical failures in 100 independent random cases. Report?
  1. Failure probability is zero
  2. Safety is proven
  3. Zero observed; one-sided 95% upper bound about 3% under the model
Your answer: Correct. An unobserved failure can still occur. The bound depends on the sampling and independence assumptions.
