Appendix A

Math and notation you will use

A compact reference for the formulas in the lessons. Each formula names its target and assumptions.

Read the symbols in words

SymbolMeaning
nNumber of observations or trials; identify the unit
kAttempts in a reliability event, or repeats when explicitly defined
p, p̂A success probability and its estimate from observed data
x̄, sSample mean and sample standard deviation
ΣAdd the indexed quantities
E[X], Var(X)Expectation and variance of a random variable
P(A | B)Probability of A given B
α, δA test’s error threshold and a specified effect size
zqq quantile of the standard normal distribution

Use probabilities on a zero-to-one scale in formulas: three percentage points is 0.03. Multiply a difference by 100 to report it in percentage points. A hat marks an estimate; a bar marks an average.

Mean, variance, and standard error

x̄ = Σxᵢ / n
s² = Σ(xᵢ−x̄)² / (n−1)
SE(x̄) ≈ s/√n

Add and divide to form the mean. Square deviations from that mean and divide by n−1 to form sample variance. Its square root measures individual-observation spread; dividing that by √n estimates uncertainty in the mean for independent, identically distributed observations with finite variance. See lesson 05.

Wilson interval

p̂ = successes/n; z = 1.9599639845 for a two-sided 95% interval
D = 1 + z²/n
Center = (p̂ + z²/(2n))/D
Half-width = z√[p̂(1−p̂)/n + z²/(4n²)]/D
Endpoints = Center ± Half-width

The center is adjusted away from the raw observed proportion, and the interval is not generally symmetric around p̂. This is a binomial score interval, assuming an appropriate iid binary sample. It does not model clustering. [NIST: proportions] See lesson 06.

Paired differences

dᵢ = Bᵢ − Aᵢ; estimated change = Σdᵢ/n
Estimated SE(change) = sd/√n

Compute each pair’s difference first and analyze those differences. A normal approximation may be suitable for many independent pairs; a t procedure makes its own distributional assumptions. A task or cluster bootstrap is another approximate approach when its unit matches the design. See lesson 08.

Planning an independent-pair comparison

n ≈ ceiling([(z1−α/2 + zpower)sd/δ]²)

Divide the assumed difference SD by the desired detectable effect, scale by the test and power thresholds, square, and round up. At alpha 0.05 and power 0.8, the two z values are about 1.960 and 0.842. This plans detection against zero under a large-sample model. See lesson 10.

The lab’s quantile convention

Sort n values and index from zero. For quantile q, calculate h = (n−1)q. Linearly interpolate between the values at floor(h) and ceiling(h). If h is an integer, use that value. This type-7 convention applies to the lab’s percentile bootstrap and posterior-draw intervals; another convention can shift endpoints in small samples.

Reliability and zero failures

At least one success in k iid attempts: 1−(1−p)ᵏ
Every attempt succeeds: pᵏ
Zero failures in n iid trials: one-sided 95% upper q = 1−0.051/n

Complement all failures to obtain at-least-one success; multiply success probabilities to obtain all-success probability. Solve the no-failure probability for a failure-rate upper bound. These are different questions, and all require the stated independence model. See lesson 19.
