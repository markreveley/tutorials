Appendix D

Reading map and next steps

Read more when a real evaluation question needs it. The course stands alone; these sources add depth.

Choose a route through this course

  • First statistics course: read 00–23 in order and predict each reasoning-check answer before revealing it.
  • Review a result this week: read 01, 04–09, 11–14, and 23; return for sample-size and selection lessons before planning the next run.
  • Design an experiment: read 01, 04, 10–14, 17–21, then fill the report template before collecting outcomes.

Foundations and calculation

The NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook gives formal accounts of mean intervals, proportion intervals, and paired observations. Read after lessons 05–09. Reproduce one formula with the local lab before moving to another method.

SciPy’s bootstrap documentation is useful when moving from the teaching implementation to a maintained scientific library. Pay attention to paired indices, the chosen interval method, and warnings. A library default is not an analysis plan.

Statistics in evals

Miller’s Adding Error Bars to Evals is the focused next reading for task variation, repeated answers, paired contrasts, and planning. Bring the distinctions from lessons 01 and 11: fixed task sets, new task populations, and independent groups need different interpretations.

Chen et al., Evaluating Large Language Models Trained on Code, section 2.1, develops the pass@k estimator. Compare its sampling event with your application’s output-selection and retry policy.

Statistical judgment

The ASA p-value principles are a short companion to lesson 09. Practice rewriting a threshold-only result as an estimate, uncertainty statement, and practical decision.

For ongoing monitoring, Howard and colleagues’ confidence-sequence paper gives the formal target for time-uniform inference. It is advanced reading. Use lesson 18 to recognize the need; do not deploy a sequential method solely from its name.

Grader and forecast quality

The scikit-learn guides to metrics and calibration explain implementation conventions. Check positive-label orientation, weighting, undefined metrics, and independent validation.

The Beta–Binomial posterior derivation lets you verify lesson 16 algebraically. For repeated heterogeneous tasks, the next topic is hierarchical modeling, not treating every attempt as an iid draw by habit.

Connect statistics back to the system

  • Understanding LLM Evals: measurement validity, grader design, contamination, and evaluation practice.
  • Networking for LLM Evals: deadlines, retries, concurrency, and missing results.
  • Linux for Evals Engineers: the execution environment, reproducibility, and harness integrity.

These courses address different layers of the same evidence chain. Sound statistics depends on observations that were collected and graded under the policy you claim to have measured.
