Module 3 · Respect the data structure

Missing outcomes, timeouts, and selection

A result that is absent from the file is still part of the experiment you scheduled.

Start with a task ledger

Before execution, create the list of scheduled tasks and planned attempts. Afterward, reconcile every expected outcome against it. Completed failures, timeouts, cancelled work, harness errors, missing logs, and excluded tasks are different statuses. Collapsing them into a blank field loses the evidence needed to interpret the score.

A missing outcome is not automatically a zero, and it is not automatically eligible for deletion. The correct handling depends on the estimand. A delivered-service success metric can count an exhausted timeout budget as unsuccessful delivery. A separate capability analysis may need to distinguish that from a task image that never started.

Why complete cases can mislead

Suppose A passes 70 of 100 scheduled tasks and all attempts finish. B passes 72, fails 18, and times out on 10. B’s completed-only rate is 80%, while A’s is 70%. Under scheduled-task success, B’s rate is 72%, an observed gain of two points. Excluding B’s timeouts creates an eight-point change in its own reported rate.

If slow failures are more likely to go missing, the observed subset is selected by the outcome process. Independence of tasks does not fix that selection. Compare missingness rates by version, task category, input length, and run conditions; investigate traces for the missing cases.

Use simple sensitivity bounds

When ten of 100 scheduled binary outcomes are genuinely unknown and 72 known outcomes passed, the scheduled pass rate lies between 72% and 82% depending on those ten labels. This is a missing-data range, not a sampling confidence interval. It identifies how much the missing outcomes alone could change the answer.

Worst case = known passes / scheduled count
Best case = (known passes + unknown outcomes) / scheduled count

Assign every unknown a failure for the lower bound and a success for the upper bound. If a decision changes across these possibilities, recovering outcomes or justifying a missingness model may be more urgent than collecting more unrelated tasks.

A timeout is partial timing information

If a task is terminated at 60 seconds, you know it did not finish within the allowed window. You do not know its natural completion time. That is censoring: you have partial information rather than a complete duration.

You can legitimately report observed wall time consumed under the 60-second policy, including terminated attempts. That target differs from eventual completion time with no deadline. Do not replace every timeout with 60 and label the resulting percentile “uncensored completion latency.” Choose a policy metric or use an appropriate time-to-event analysis with explicit assumptions.

Rerun rules belong in the protocol

Rerunning only the candidate’s failed tasks until they pass changes its effective attempt budget. Rerunning a documented broken environment under a symmetric rule can be justified, but retain the original status and reason. Report the system’s behavior under the policy you actually executed.

In eval work

Connect this audit to the networking lesson on retries and the Linux harness lesson. Infrastructure behavior can change which outcomes enter a statistical table.

Pitfall

Encoding missing as zero and then claiming you made no assumptions. That encoding is a scoring policy; write it down and ensure it answers the intended question.

Check your understanding

A candidate looks faster after you remove every timeout. What evidence would distinguish real speed improvement from selection?

Reveal the reasoning

Compare scheduled counts, timeout fractions, task-level paired elapsed times under a common policy, and the types of tasks removed. Report successful-only latency as a conditional metric alongside completion rates. Recover missing records before inferring an unconditional speed gain.

Practice

For 200 scheduled tasks, 130 known passes, 50 known failures, and 20 unknown outcomes, calculate completed-case accuracy and worst/best scheduled-task rates. Choose what to report.

What to look for

Completed-case accuracy is 130/180 ≈ 72.2%. The scheduled-task range is 65% to 75% before sampling uncertainty. Report all counts, why the twenty are unknown, and the analysis or recovery policy. Do not call this range a 95% interval.
