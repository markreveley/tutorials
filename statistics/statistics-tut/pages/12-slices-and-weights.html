Module 3 · Respect the data structure

Slices, weights, and misleading averages

Decide whose tasks the average represents and inspect important subgroups without turning every slice into a discovery.

An average has weights

A stratum is a defined subgroup, such as task type or language. A slice is a practical label for a subgroup you inspect. An overall average combines subgroup results using weights, whether you choose them explicitly or let dataset counts choose them implicitly.

Suppose a fixture includes 80 routine and 40 sensitive tasks. A pooled task mean gives routine tasks two thirds of the weight. An equal-category macro-average gives each category one half. A traffic-weighted mean uses the deployment mix. These are different estimands, not interchangeable formatting options.

Overall change = w × routine change + (1−w) × sensitive change

Multiply each category’s change by its target proportion and add. The weights must describe the population or normative priority you intend to measure. If categories overlap, this simple partition formula cannot be used without resolving double counting.

A gain that hides a regression

In the clustered fixture, the routine category improves by about 23.33 percentage points; the sensitive category worsens by about 16.67 points. With two-thirds routine weight, the overall change is +10 points. Equal category weighting gives about +3.33 points. A hypothetical workload with 20% routine and 80% sensitive work gives about −8.67 points.

All three summaries use the same category changes. Before selecting one, ask what population or priority the report is supposed to represent. A high-impact category can also have a guardrail independent of the average. A weighted benefit elsewhere need not compensate for a critical failure.

Different mixes can reverse a comparison

SystemEasy tasksHard tasksOverall
A81/90 = 90%2/10 = 20%83/100 = 83%
B19/20 = 95%24/80 = 30%43/100 = 43%

B’s observed within-category rates are higher, but its overall score is lower because it saw mostly hard tasks. This is a constructed example of a mix-driven reversal. It does not prove a causal advantage within categories: different task selection may still confound those comparisons. A matched task set and common weights provide a more interpretable design.

Weight the uncertainty calculation too

If the sampling design fixes category counts, a stratified bootstrap typically resamples independent units within categories and then applies the target weights. Do not bootstrap a pooled file and assume it reproduces a design that intentionally fixed stratum counts. If repositories span categories, preserving repository dependence and category design together requires a suitable method.

The course CLI reports the included sample’s equal-task mean and can filter one slice. It does not implement general survey weights or a stratified design. Use the arithmetic above to understand sensitivity; do not attach the CLI’s unweighted interval to a newly weighted estimate.

In eval work

Predeclare a primary aggregate and a small set of important guardrails. Use other slices to find hypotheses for follow-up. A discovered weak slice can be operationally important even when it is not a confirmatory statistical finding.

Pitfall

Searching dozens of slices and presenting the largest observed gain as if it were planned. The selection process affects how confidently you can generalize that result.

Check your understanding

Your eval oversamples rare, sensitive tasks to get more information. Must you discard those extra tasks to report production performance?

Reveal the reasoning

No. Keep the data and use a design-aware weighted estimator for a specified production mixture, with uncertainty that respects the sampling design. Also report the sensitive slice directly. The weights and the extra diagnostic coverage serve different purposes.

Practice

Inspect the two category changes and their group counts. Compute the overall change at routine weights of 2/3, 1/2, and 1/5.

python3 labs/stats_lab.py compare --data labs/data/clustered_tasks.jsonl --unit cluster --slice routine
python3 labs/stats_lab.py compare --data labs/data/clustered_tasks.jsonl --unit cluster --slice sensitive
What to look for

The changes are approximately +10, +3.33, and −8.67 points. The sensitive-slice interval is based on only ten independent repositories in this teaching model. Do not treat an exploratory slice interval as automatically adjusted for all slices you might inspect.
