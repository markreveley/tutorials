Module 0 · Ask a measurable question

Read the observations before the average

Understand the shape of your data and what an average hides before attaching an error bar.

What does one row mean?

A useful eval table retains task ID, related-group ID, system version, attempt number, outcome, grader version, elapsed time, and failure status. Never assume one row equals one independent piece of evidence. A row might be a tool call, an attempt, a task summary, or an entire run.

Our JSONL files have one JSON object per line and one task per object. Fields a and b contain binary outcomes for each version’s attempts. In the first dataset each list has one element. In the second it has three. The cluster_id records a shared repository, and slice records the task category. That structure is intentional: it keeps relationships visible.

Mean, median, and spread

The mean is the sum divided by the count. The median is the middle ordered value; for an even count we average the two middle values. Consider five hypothetical runtimes in seconds:

Ordered runtimes: 1 1 2 2 24 Mean: (1 + 1 + 2 + 2 + 24) / 5 = 6 Median: middle value = 2

The mean is useful for total resource planning: these five jobs consume 30 task-seconds in total. The median describes a typical central observation. Neither is a substitute for the other. Reporting just the median hides the 24-second task; reporting just the mean makes it easy to imagine every task taking about six seconds.

A quantile marks a position in an ordered distribution. A 95th percentile, or p95, is near the upper tail. Sample quantile conventions differ. The lab uses linear interpolation at index (n−1)q in a zero-indexed sorted list. For the five runtimes, q = 0.95 gives index 3.8 and a value of 19.6 seconds. A nearest-rank convention gives 24. Neither creates enough observations to characterize a real p95 well.

Know the score’s scale

With pass=1 and fail=0, the mean is the pass rate. A fractional score such as 0.6 might mean three of five rubric points, an average of repeated binary outcomes, or a grader’s subjective rating. Those meanings matter. A binomial calculation for 0/1 trials does not automatically apply to fractional task scores.

Ordinal ratings such as poor/fair/good have an ordering but no guaranteed equal spacing. Turning them into 1/2/3 and averaging imposes a spacing assumption. A useful report retains the category counts and rubric so readers can assess whether the summary is meaningful.

Investigate unusual observations

An outlier may be a timestamp bug, a legitimate difficult task, or a distinct failure mechanism. Find its trace. Correct documented measurement errors; keep real slow tasks in a workload metric that is supposed to include them. If you report a trimmed mean, define the trimming rule and explain that it estimates something different from total average runtime.

In eval work

Compare distributions and counts by version and task category before inference. A sudden score increase accompanied by fewer rows, lost IDs, or a missing hard slice is a data-integrity lead, not a statistical discovery.

Pitfall

A small standard error around a wrong aggregate is precise reporting of the wrong quantity. First establish the unit, scale, inclusion rules, and unusual cases.

Check your understanding

An agent’s median latency improves while total runtime and timeout rate get worse. Can both reports be true?

Reveal the reasoning

Yes. More than half of cases may have become faster while a smaller tail became much slower or failed to complete. Inspect full timings and failure status. A successful-only median can also conceal excluded timeouts.

Practice

Open the data file in a text editor and inspect a few records. Run the small resampling demonstration, then explain what three outcomes inside one task would mean for the task count.

python3 labs/stats_lab.py compare --resamples 100
What to look for

Three outcomes are three attempts on one task. The file retains task identity so averaging attempts or resampling related tasks does not require guessing relationships from row order.
