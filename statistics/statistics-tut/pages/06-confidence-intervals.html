Module 1 · Understand uncertainty

Confidence intervals and what they cover

An error bar is a statement about a procedure under assumptions, not a guarantee that the benchmark measured the right thing.

Imagine repeating the procedure

You observe 72 passes in 100 independent sampled task-and-attempt pairs. The estimate is 72%. A confidence interval adds a range constructed by a stated method. A nominal 95% method aims to contain the fixed target in approximately 95% of repeated experiments under its assumptions. Some methods are exact or conservative; approximate methods can miss that nominal rate.

After you observe the data, the interval’s endpoints are fixed. In this frequentist interpretation, “there is a 95% probability the parameter is inside this realized interval” is not the definition. You can still use the interval to express the precision of the procedure and the values reasonably compatible with the data and model. [NIST: means]

A useful interval for binary outcomes

A common shortcut is p̂ ± 1.96 × √(p̂(1−p̂)/n). It treats the estimated rate as approximately normal with a plug-in standard error. Near zero or one, or with small counts, it behaves badly. Ten passes out of ten gives an estimated SE of zero and the interval [100%, 100%], which expresses certainty the data do not warrant.

The Wilson interval comes from a different construction: invert a score test rather than attaching a symmetric margin to the observed rate. Its endpoints stay between zero and one. The math reference gives the full formula and definitions. [NIST: proportions]

72 / 10072.0%62.5% to 79.9%
10 / 10100.0%72.2% to 100.0%

These numbers are calculated by the included lab. Ten successes leave considerable uncertainty about the underlying success probability. Wilson intervals are approximate binomial intervals, not universal solutions for clusters or graded scores.

Identify the interval’s object

An interval for the mean success rate differs from a prediction interval for a future measurement. The first concerns an unknown average. The second must also accommodate variation in the future observation itself. A narrow estimate of average latency does not mean every future task will finish near that average.

A 95% interval is also not the central 95% of your raw task scores. With binary outcomes, the latter will often span 0 and 1 regardless of how precisely the mean is known. Ask which distribution each interval summarizes.

What is outside the error bar?

At n = 100 and p = 0.7, enumerating every possible binomial outcome gives this Wilson procedure about 93.72% actual coverage. The nominal 95% label is an approximation, and finite discrete samples need not achieve it exactly.

A task-sampling interval does not generally include grader mistakes, omitted languages, changing model versions, hidden retries, or future workload shift. Those require better measurement, additional data, or explicit modeling. Listing these limitations is part of interpreting the result, not a reason to abandon uncertainty estimates.

In eval work

Report the numerator and denominator, estimate, interval method, confidence level, sampling unit, and intended population. “72/100; 95% Wilson interval 62.5–79.9%, assuming independent sampled cases” is substantially more useful than “72% ± error.”

Pitfall

An interval that excludes zero does not prove the model is useful, and an interval that includes zero does not prove no effect exists. Both the effect size and what you would act on matter.

Check your understanding

A team reports 100% success on ten cases and says its confidence interval has zero width. What likely happened?

Reveal the reasoning

It may have used a plug-in normal standard error, which collapses when the observed rate is one. Use an appropriate binomial interval if the iid model applies, and inspect task selection and dependence. Perfect observed performance does not establish perfect future performance.

Practice

Calculate two intervals, then observe their coverage over simulated experiments. Explain what the coverage percentage counts.

python3 labs/stats_lab.py rate 72 100
python3 labs/stats_lab.py rate 10 10
python3 labs/stats_lab.py sample --p 0.7 --trials 100
What to look for

Coverage counts experiments whose calculated interval contains the simulator’s known p. It does not count the fraction of raw outcomes inside the interval. You can also vary counts in the interactive calculators.
