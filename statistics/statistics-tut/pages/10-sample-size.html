Module 2 · Compare systems

Sample size: plan the precision you need

Choose a meaningful effect and a defensible variance assumption before spending your eval budget.

There is no universal task count

“How many examples do I need?” is incomplete. To estimate one rate to a chosen precision, you need a margin of error and an outcome model. To detect a difference, you need its size, a test, an error threshold, desired power, and a variance assumption. Clustering and stratification can change the calculation.

For an iid binary rate near 50%, the approximate 95% margin of error is 1.96 × √(0.25/n). Solving for a margin of five percentage points gives about 385 observations. Near 50% is a conservative planning choice for Bernoulli variance; it does not make a convenience sample representative.

Plan for paired differences

For an approximately normal mean of independent paired differences, a common large-sample planning approximation is:

n ≈ [(z1−α/2 + zpower) × sd / δ]²

The numerator combines the two-sided test threshold and desired-power threshold, then multiplies by the assumed standard deviation of task differences. Divide by the effect δ you want to detect and square. Round upward. A z value is a quantile of the standard normal distribution; the lab computes it. This is an approximation, not an exact design guarantee. [Error bars]

With difference SD 0.5, a three-point effect (δ = 0.03), alpha 0.05, and power 0.8, the lab plans 2,181 independent pairs. The assumed SD belongs to B−A, not to A alone. A pilot on representative, independently selected units can inform it, but a noisy pilot should motivate sensitivity checks.

Ask what could change the answer

Halving the target effect approximately quadruples the task requirement when other assumptions stay fixed. Strong positive pairing can lower difference variance. Related tasks can reduce the effective information. A tiny rare-failure slice may require its own plan even if the overall average has ample precision.

Run the planning calculation with several plausible difference SDs. If the budget works only under the most optimistic assumption, state that risk before collecting data. For complex clustered, weighted, or sequential designs, simulate the actual proposed sampling and analysis rather than applying an independent-pair formula unchanged.

Tasks, attempts, and grader effort

Budget for distinct tasks, repeated attempts, and validation of the grader. If the main uncertainty is how performance varies between repositories, adding independent repositories may help more than rerunning one repository a hundred times. If each observed task’s behavior is very unstable, repeats can help. If grader labels are unreliable, more unreviewed labels can precisely reproduce the wrong score.

The formula above plans rejection of a zero-effect null when the true effect is δ. It does not plan proof that the effect exceeds a separate deployment margin. To support a gain above three points when you expect five, the gap to the decision boundary is only two points; use a design matching that hypothesis.

In eval work

Record the smallest effect that changes your decision, the assumed paired SD, how that assumption was obtained, and the planned stopping point. A reviewer can then assess whether “no clear difference” was an expected possibility.

Pitfall

Computing “observed power” from the final observed effect adds little to the result. After data collection, report the interval and what important effects it does or does not rule out.

Check your understanding

You planned to detect a six-point effect but now care about three points. Can the same task count promise the same power?

Reveal the reasoning

Generally no. Under the same approximate independent-pair variance model, halving the effect needs about four times as many pairs. Revisit the budget or accept a less precise decision; do not hide the changed requirement.

Practice

Predict which plan requires the most pairs. Run all three and explain why a smaller difference SD changes the budget.

python3 labs/stats_lab.py power --delta 0.06 --sd 0.5
python3 labs/stats_lab.py power --delta 0.03 --sd 0.5
python3 labs/stats_lab.py power --delta 0.03 --sd 0.35
What to look for

A smaller target effect raises the requirement; a smaller difference SD lowers it. The results count independent pairs. They are not permissions to substitute correlated attempts for new tasks.
