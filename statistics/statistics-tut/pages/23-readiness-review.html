Module 5 · Make defensible decisions

Readiness review: defend the decision

Finish by reviewing an eval as an engineer who must explain both the evidence and the next action.

Your review brief

A teammate wants to replace agent A with B. They report a ten-point average gain from the clustered fixture and say the lower interval endpoint exceeds +3 points. The predeclared primary rule requires a gain above +3 points, assessed using the lower endpoint of a 95% interval under the intended independent-repository design. Sensitive tasks have a separate guardrail against deterioration greater than two points, to be assessed by a predeclared appropriate method before any real deployment.

The teammate used the task bootstrap. There are thirty repositories, four tasks per repository, and three attempts per model per task. The fixture’s sensitive category contains ten repositories. They also suggest that B could run with more retries because its best-of-three performance would look better.

Your assignment is to review that argument. Use the supplied data and earlier lessons. This is a synthetic training case; the output is a review, not authorization for any real deployment.

Write a one-page decision memo

  1. State the population, unit, score, weighting, and run policy the analysis assumes.
  2. Report the hierarchy of counts, paired estimate, and appropriate interval.
  3. Explain whether the primary evidence clears the declared threshold.
  4. Report the sensitive-category observation with its independent-unit count and inference limitations.
  5. Identify missing evidence about grader quality, task selection, cost, and execution conditions.
  6. Choose a next action and explain which uncertainty it will reduce.

A strong memo distinguishes “observed in this file,” “inferred under this model,” and “not established.” It should be possible for another engineer to reproduce the observed quantities and challenge the assumptions without guessing what you meant.

You are ready for practical introductory eval analysis when you can explain each row using the case, not merely define the vocabulary. Advanced designs may still require statistical review. Recognizing when your current tools do not match the design is itself a useful skill.

What to learn next when work demands it

For nested and crossed dependencies, study multilevel models and design-aware resampling. For variable user exposure and missing outcomes, study survey sampling and missing-data methods. For online launches, study randomized experiments, interference, and sequential inference. For censored completion times, study survival analysis. The reading map links each next step to a concrete need.

You do not need to memorize every method to review an eval intelligently. You do need to ask which observations were independently collected, which target is being estimated, what assumptions the uncertainty covers, and how the decision was selected. Those questions transfer across methods.

In eval work

The final artifact should leave a teammate knowing what to do next: inspect specific failures, collect independent cases, audit the grader, or proceed under an already justified policy. A score without that reasoning leaves the hardest part unfinished.

Pitfall

Treating a positive aggregate as permission to average away a guardrail regression. A guardrail is a separate decision requirement precisely because the overall mean can hide important failures.

Check your understanding

What should your memo conclude about the teammate’s argument?

Reveal the reasoning

The observed aggregate gain is +10 points. The repository bootstrap interval is about +1.11 to +18.89, so its lower endpoint does not clear +3. The sensitive slice declines about 16.67 points; its ten-group exploratory interval is not a complete confirmatory guardrail analysis, but the observed regression is a clear investigation priority. More retries change the evaluated policy. Do not claim the proposed evidence establishes readiness to replace A.

What to look for

A defensible follow-up freezes policy and grader, investigates sensitive failures, and gathers more independent, relevant repositories under a planned design. If the candidate is revised, confirm it on fresh held-out groups. Merely adding bootstrap draws or hiding the sensitive slice does not solve the problem.
