Module 5 · Make defensible decisions

Reliability, retries, and rare failures

Measure the success event your application needs, and avoid reading zero observed failures as proof of zero risk.

One success and repeated success are different

For a task with independent attempts and success probability p, the chance of at least one success in k attempts is 1−(1−p)ᵏ. The chance every attempt succeeds is pᵏ. At p = 0.8 and k = 3, these are 0.992 and 0.512. A system can be good at eventually finding a solution while remaining inconsistent.

In eval terminology, pass@k measures at-least-one success, averaged across tasks under a specified sampling policy. All-k success is sometimes written pass^k. Define the event in words because similar notation can conceal very different operational requirements.

Estimate within each task first

If you have n sampled attempts on a task with c successes, the standard pass@k estimator for k ≤ n is 1 − C(n−c,k)/C(n,k). C(n,k) counts size-k subsets. The ratio is the fraction of subsets that contain only failures; subtract it from one. Chen and colleagues use this estimator for code evaluation. [HumanEval]

The analogous all-k estimator is C(c,k)/C(n,k), the fraction of subsets consisting only of successes. These are estimators of the corresponding iid-attempt probabilities, not uncertainty intervals. Average task-level estimates using your declared task weights.

Do not raise the overall mean pass rate to k when tasks have different success probabilities. For two equally weighted tasks with p values 1 and 0, the average p is 0.5. Their average all-two-success probability is (1²+0²)/2 = 0.5, while the square of their average is 0.25. Nonlinear transformations and averaging do not generally commute.

The retry policy changes the experiment

Independent sampling on a reset task differs from adaptive retries that inspect failure feedback, retain state, or change prompts. Shared outages also break independent-attempt assumptions. Evaluate the full deployed retry policy when that is the decision target.

At-least-one success assumes you can identify or verify the successful candidate. If an application must choose one output without a reliable verifier, pass@k alone does not establish delivered success. Include selection cost, verification errors, and total attempt budget.

Zero failures still leaves an upper bound

For zero failures in n independent trials with a constant failure probability q, the chance of seeing no failures is (1−q)ⁿ. Set this to 0.05 and solve for a one-sided 95% upper bound:

qupper = 1 − 0.051/n ≈ 3/n

The approximation is useful for large n. At n = 100, the exact bound is about 2.95%; at n = 1,000, about 0.299%. This derivation is the zero-failure specialization of exact binomial bounds. [NIST: proportions] It assumes a fixed planned count and the stated sampling model.

To get this upper bound below 0.1% with zero observed failures requires at least 2,995 independent trials. That does not certify safety: if tasks were handpicked adversarial cases, the rate concerns their selection process, not ordinary traffic. Dependence, missed failures, and uncovered hazards are separate limitations.

In eval work

For an important failure class, report event definition, observed count, exposure unit, sampling policy, detection process, and an appropriate bound. Keep failure severity visible alongside frequency.

Pitfall

Taking a bootstrap of a zero-failure sample, getting a zero-width interval, and announcing the failure is impossible. A resampling method cannot reveal an event absent from its empirical distribution.

Check your understanding

An agent succeeds at least once in five tries on nearly every task. Does that establish reliable one-shot use?

Reveal the reasoning

No. It measures a five-attempt event, which can be high even when individual attempts often fail. Specify the deployment policy, verify how a successful result is selected, and measure the relevant one-shot or repeated-success event.

Practice

Predict which is larger: at-least-one or all-three success. Then inspect how the zero-failure upper bound changes with ten times more independent trials.

python3 labs/stats_lab.py reliability 8 10 3
python3 labs/stats_lab.py rare 100
python3 labs/stats_lab.py rare 1000
What to look for

For eight observed passes out of ten, the at-least-one-three estimator is 1 because only two sampled attempts failed; the all-three estimator is 56/120 ≈ 0.4667. That point estimate of 1 is not proof of certainty. The failure upper bound shrinks by roughly a factor of ten.
