Module 4 · Measure the measuring instrument

A Bayesian view of uncertainty

Update an explicit prior with observed outcomes, and learn which assumptions a posterior probability still depends on.

A different kind of probability statement

Frequentist confidence intervals describe repeated procedures with a fixed target. A Bayesian analysis represents uncertainty about a parameter with a probability distribution. After observing data, the posterior combines a prior distribution with a likelihood describing how the data arise.

Prior uncertainty × likelihood of observed data ↓ normalize Posterior uncertainty

This framework lets you ask “under this model and prior, what is the probability that the pass rate exceeds 0.8?” The conditions in that sentence are essential. A posterior is not an assumption-free probability of real-world success.

The Beta–Binomial example

Let p be one stable Bernoulli success probability. A Beta distribution has two positive shape parameters, α and β, and lives between zero and one. A Beta(1,1) prior has uniform density on that interval. It is one possible weak prior for this parameterization, not a uniquely neutral choice.

With s successes in n independent trials, the posterior is:

p | data ∼ Beta(α + s, β + n − s)

Add successes to the first prior shape and failures to the second. This follows by multiplying the Beta prior density by the binomial likelihood and collecting powers of p and 1−p. [Beta update] The posterior mean is (α+s)/(α+β+n), which blends the prior mean with the observed rate.

For eight successes in ten trials, Beta(1,1) becomes Beta(9,3), with mean 0.75. A Beta(8,2) prior becomes Beta(16,4), with mean 0.8. Neither calculation changes the observations; the assumptions about uncertainty before seeing them changed.

Read a credible interval

A 95% credible interval contains 95% of posterior probability under the model and prior. An equal-tail interval leaves 2.5% in each tail. It can differ from a frequentist interval in both interpretation and endpoints. Do not silently switch between them when reporting results.

The lab draws from the posterior using Python’s Beta sampler. The posterior mean is calculated exactly by the formula above; reported credible endpoints and threshold probabilities are Monte Carlo approximations. More posterior draws reduce simulation error, not uncertainty from having only ten observed trials.

Vary the prior, inspect the model

Try a weak prior, a more concentrated optimistic prior, and a skeptical prior. If the decision changes substantially, report that sensitivity. As appropriate data accumulate, prior influence often decreases, but even abundant data cannot repair a likelihood that ignores dependence or a systematically biased task sample.

This one-parameter model treats observations as iid Bernoulli draws with one marginal p. Repeated attempts on heterogeneous fixed tasks need more structure. For paired A/B data, independently drawing two posterior rates can discard the dependence that pairing captures. A Bayesian approach must preserve the experimental structure just as a bootstrap must.

In eval work

Posterior threshold probabilities can support a decision when paired with explicit utilities and a defensible model. Include the prior, likelihood, sampling assumptions, and sensitivity analysis in the report.

Pitfall

Choosing a prior after looking at results so the posterior clears a launch threshold. That reuses the data as unstated evidence and undermines the interpretation you are presenting.

Check your understanding

The lab reports a high posterior probability that p exceeds your threshold. Does that validate the grader or task selection?

Reveal the reasoning

No. It is conditional on the supplied observations, likelihood, and prior. A biased grader or unrepresentative sample can produce a concentrated posterior for the wrong target. Validate the measuring process separately.

Practice

Predict which prior gives the highest posterior mean. Compare threshold probabilities and intervals, then repeat each with 80 successes in 100 trials.

python3 labs/stats_lab.py bayes 8 10 --alpha 1 --beta 1 --threshold 0.8
python3 labs/stats_lab.py bayes 8 10 --alpha 8 --beta 2 --threshold 0.8
python3 labs/stats_lab.py bayes 8 10 --alpha 2 --beta 8 --threshold 0.8
What to look for

The optimistic prior pushes the small-sample posterior upward; the skeptical prior pulls it downward. With more data at the same observed rate, the prior has less influence. The counts must still satisfy the likelihood’s independence assumptions.
