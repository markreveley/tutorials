Reference

Glossary

Definitions in the language of eval work. Linked terms in lessons also show keyboard-accessible tooltips.

All-k success

The probability every one of k attempts succeeds on a task, averaged over tasks; sometimes written pass^k.

Benjamini–Hochberg

Sort m p-values, find the largest i with p(i) ≤ (i/m)q, and reject the i smallest; it controls the false discovery rate at q under independence or positive dependence.

Bernoulli

A distribution for a binary outcome: one with probability p, zero otherwise.

Bias

A systematic discrepancy. Estimator bias means its expected value differs from its target; measurement or selection bias can change the target being measured.

Binomial

The distribution of the number of successes in a fixed number of independent Bernoulli trials sharing one success probability.

Bonferroni

Testing each of m hypotheses at alpha/m, which bounds the familywise error rate at alpha when every individual p-value is valid.

Bootstrap

Approximate sampling variation by drawing new samples with replacement from observed independent units and recomputing the statistic.

Brier score

The average squared difference between a forecast probability and a binary outcome; lower is better.

Calibration

Agreement between stated probabilities and observed event frequencies on the relevant population.

Censoring

Partial observation of a value, such as knowing runtime exceeded a timeout without observing its eventual duration.

Cluster

A group of observations that can share dependence, such as tasks from one repository.

Conditional probability

The probability of an event after restricting attention to a stated condition.

Confidence interval

An interval from a procedure designed to cover a fixed target at a stated long-run rate under its assumptions.

Confidence sequence

A sequence of intervals designed for simultaneous coverage across the permitted observation times under stated assumptions.

Confounding

Mixing the effect of interest with other differences, so the comparison cannot isolate the claimed cause.

Confusion matrix

Counts crossing reference labels with predicted labels, exposing true and false positives and negatives.

Correlation

A number between −1 and +1 describing how two paired measurements vary together; zero means no linear or monotone association of the kind measured, not independence.

Covariance

A measure of whether two quantities tend to vary together; it enters the variance of their difference.

Coverage

The probability that an interval-producing procedure contains its target over repetitions of the specified experiment.

Credible interval

An interval containing a specified amount of posterior probability under the chosen model and prior.

Denominator

The count or weight being divided by. Changing it can change the question a rate answers.

Distribution shift

A change in the population or conditions between evaluation and the use to which its results are applied.

Effect size

The magnitude of a difference or relationship on a specified scale, such as percentage points.

Equivalence

Evidence that a difference lies within predeclared lower and upper practical margins; failure to reject zero does not establish it.

Estimand

The exact population quantity you want to estimate, including the population, run policy, score, and weighting.

Estimate

A number calculated from observed data to learn about an estimand.

Eval

A defined procedure for measuring a system on tasks using specified scoring rules.

Expectation

A probability-weighted average of possible values; it need not itself be an outcome.

False discovery rate

The expected fraction of false rejections among rejections, with the fraction defined as zero if nothing is rejected.

Familywise error

The probability of at least one false rejection in a specified family of hypotheses.

Held-out split

A division of observations into a part used to choose or fit and a part reserved to score the choice; the reserved part gives an unbiased estimate only while it stays unused for selection.

Holdout

Data reserved for evaluation after choices have been fixed; repeatedly tuning against it spends its independence.

Independence

A property under which learning one outcome does not change the probability distribution of another.

Likelihood

The probability or density of the observed data viewed as a function of a model parameter.

Macro-average

An average that gives equal weight to groups, rather than weighting by each group’s observation count.

Mean

Sum of the values divided by their count; a binary mean is the fraction of passes.

Median

A central ordered value; for an even count, this course averages the two central values.

Micro-average

An aggregate formed by pooling the underlying contributions, such as total correct divided by total attempted.

Minimum detectable effect

The effect a planned design can detect with stated power and error threshold under its assumptions.

Monte Carlo error

Numerical uncertainty caused by using finitely many random simulations; distinct from uncertainty due to having limited evaluation data.

Noninferiority

A claim that a candidate’s loss is smaller than a predeclared acceptable margin, supported by an appropriate one-sided analysis.

Null distribution

The distribution a test statistic would have if the null hypothesis held; a p-value locates the observed statistic within it.

Null hypothesis

A specified statistical claim against which a test evaluates compatibility, often no average change.

Optional stopping

Choosing when to stop from observed results; valid inference must account for the rule used.

Outlier

An observation unusually far from others; investigate its origin before deciding whether it belongs in the analysis.

P-value

Under a specified null model, the probability of a test statistic at least as extreme as observed, using the stated direction or tail rule.

Paired comparison

An analysis that retains the relationship between measurements on the same task or matched unit.

Pareto frontier

Choices for which no other available choice is at least as good on every stated objective and better on at least one.

Pass@k

The probability at least one of k attempts succeeds on a task, averaged over tasks under a specified sampling policy.

Pearson correlation

The sum of products of paired deviations from the means divided by the root of the product of the summed squared deviations; it measures linear co-variation and is sensitive to extreme values.

Percentage point

An absolute difference between percentages: 72% to 78% is six percentage points.

Permutation test

A test that recomputes a statistic after shuffling the labels relative to the other variable; the p-value is the fraction of relabelings at least as extreme as the observed one under the exchangeable-labels null.

Population

The tasks, attempts, users, or other units to which a stated result is meant to apply.

Posterior

A parameter distribution after updating a prior with a likelihood and observed data.

Power

The probability a test rejects its null under a particular alternative and experimental design.

Precision

Among predicted positive cases, the fraction actually positive according to the reference labels; distinct from statistical precision of an estimator.

Prevalence

The proportion of positive cases in a specified population; here often the failure rate.

Prior

A probability distribution expressing uncertainty about a parameter before the analyzed data.

Probability

A number between zero and one describing uncertainty about an event under a model.

Pseudoreplication

Treating dependent observations as independent evidence about a population.

Quantile

A value locating a stated fraction of a distribution; sample quantiles require a specified interpolation convention.

Random variable

A numeric quantity whose value depends on an uncertain outcome.

Recall

Among reference-positive cases, the fraction correctly detected; also called sensitivity.

Sample

The units actually observed under a stated selection process.

Sampling frame

The accessible list or process from which a sample can be selected; it may omit parts of the target population.

Screening

Testing many candidates with the same procedure in order to select a few for follow-up; the selection step changes what the surviving estimates mean.

Selection bias

A distortion that arises when the cases analyzed were chosen by a rule related to the outcome, such as reporting the best of many candidates as if it had been the only one tested.

Spearman correlation

The Pearson correlation of the ranks of two variables, with tied values sharing average ranks; it measures any monotone relationship and ignores how far apart values are.

Specificity

Among reference-negative cases, the fraction correctly predicted negative.

Standard deviation

The square root of variance; it describes spread on the original measurement scale.

Standard error

The standard deviation of an estimator over repetitions of the stated sampling process; usually estimated from the observed data.

Stratum

A defined population subgroup sampled or analyzed separately, such as a language or task category.

Type I error

Rejecting a true null hypothesis; a test’s alpha aims to limit this frequency under its assumptions.

Type II error

Failing to reject a null when a specified alternative is true.

Variance

Average squared distance from an expectation; sample variance uses an estimated center and usually divides by n−1.

Wilson interval

A score-test interval for a binomial proportion whose endpoints remain in the allowable zero-to-one range.

Winner’s curse

The tendency of the best-looking candidate among many to have been helped by noise, so its observed effect overstates what fresh data will show.
