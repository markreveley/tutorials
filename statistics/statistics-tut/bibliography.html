Sources and provenance

Annotated bibliography

Primary papers, institutional references, and official method documentation, with the verified scope of each source.

These sources were opened and checked during authoring on 2026-09-21. The notes identify the scope verified; they do not claim that an entire source or every linked method was independently reproduced. The local experiments validate the supplied implementations and synthetic examples.

Error bars

Evan Miller (2024), Adding Error Bars to Evals, §§2–5

Verified: task-level differences for paired comparisons; clustered questions require dependence-aware uncertainty; averaging repeated answers reduces within-question variability; sample-size planning uses effect and variance assumptions.

NIST: proportions

NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook, §7.2.4.1: Confidence intervals for proportions

Verified: Wilson formula, bounded endpoints, distinction from the Wald interval, and exact binomial interval construction.

NIST: means

NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook, §1.3.5.2: Confidence limits for the mean

Verified: mean interval uses standard deviation divided by square root of sample size and a t critical value; interpretation concerns repeated interval construction.

NIST: paired

NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook, §7.3.1.1: Analysis of paired observations

Verified: form within-pair differences and analyze their mean; the paired t procedure uses the variance of those differences.

SciPy: bootstrap

SciPy documentation: scipy.stats.bootstrap

Verified: resampling with replacement, percentile/basic/BCa alternatives, paired=True shares sampled indices, and degenerate distributions need care. The local lab explicitly uses percentile intervals, not the SciPy default.

ASA

American Statistical Association (2016), Statement on statistical significance and p-values: release and six principles

Verified: p-values do not give hypothesis probabilities or effect importance; threshold-only decisions and selective reporting are inappropriate.

SciPy: binomial

SciPy documentation: scipy.stats.binomtest

Verified: test of a binomial success probability with two-sided and one-sided alternatives. The local discordance test specializes the binomial null to p=0.5.

NIST: Bonferroni

NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook, §7.4.6.3: Bonferroni’s method

Verified: allocating alpha across a specified family bounds its overall error using the Bonferroni inequality.

Confidence sequences

Howard, Ramdas, McAuliffe & Sekhon, Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences

Verified from abstract: confidence sequences provide time-uniform coverage under their assumptions. No particular implementation or sample efficiency is claimed in the course.

Metrics

scikit-learn user guide, §3.4: Metrics and scoring

Verified: confusion-matrix and precision/recall/F-score definitions; averaging schemes change what summary metrics mean. The examples and confusion matrices here are original.

Calibration

scikit-learn user guide, §1.16: Probability calibration

Verified: reliability diagrams compare forecast averages with observed frequencies; proper scores assess more than calibration alone; calibration fitting requires independent data.

Beta update

Joram Soch, The Book of Statistical Proofs: Posterior distribution for binomial observations

Verified: Beta prior with Binomial likelihood yields Beta(alpha+successes, beta+failures). Course posterior numbers are computed locally.

NIST: design

NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook, §5.3.3.2: Randomized block designs

Verified: blocking controls known nuisance factors and randomization operates within blocks. Application to agent execution order is an original course design example.

HumanEval

Chen et al. (2021), Evaluating Large Language Models Trained on Code, §2.1

Verified: pass@k estimates whether at least one of k samples passes and uses 1-C(n-c,k)/C(n,k). No model performance claims are reproduced.

Equivalence tests

statsmodels documentation: paired two-one-sided equivalence tests

Verified: equivalence is tested against specified lower and upper limits using two one-sided tests; it is not established by failing to reject a zero difference.

Agreement

scikit-learn documentation: Cohen’s kappa

Verified: kappa=(observed agreement−expected agreement)/(1−expected agreement), with expected agreement derived from empirical label marginals; weighting options for ordinal labels.

Multiple testing

statsmodels documentation: multiple-test corrections

Verified: implementations distinguish FWER and FDR procedures, including Bonferroni, Holm, Benjamini–Hochberg, and Benjamini–Yekutieli; dependence assumptions matter.
