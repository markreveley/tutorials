Module 3 · Language methods on GPT-2 small

Language II: the logit lens and direct logit attribution

Reading intermediate residual states as if they were final, decomposing a logit into component contributions, and the known ways each one misleads.

The logit lens

nostalgebraist’s 2020 observation: “GPT's probabilistic predictions are a linear function of the activations in its final layer. If one applies the same function to the activations of intermediate GPT layers, the resulting distributions make intuitive sense” [Logit lens]. Apply the final layer norm and the unembedding to blocks.L.hook_resid_post and you get, for each layer, the distribution the model would output if that layer were the last. The post’s summary: “At some point in the middle, GPT will have formed a ‘pretty good guess’ as to the next token, and the later layers seem to be refining these guesses in light of one another.”

The reference script runs it on “The capital of France is” and reports the top token, its probability, the probability of “ Paris”, and the KL divergence from the layer’s distribution to the final one:

First, the model. GPT-2 small does not answer this prompt with “ Paris”; its top token is “ now” and Paris is ranked 93rd. That is a fact about a 124M-parameter model and the course reports it rather than choosing a prompt that works. Second, the lens. The intermediate distributions are confidently wrong in a way the final one is not: block 7 puts 74% on “ now”, the final layer 5%. The KL to the final distribution falls from about 4 to 0.3 over the last three blocks, which is where the lens says the output is formed. The “pretty good guess” pattern the post describes for GPT-2 XL is not what this prompt shows for GPT-2 small; what it shows is a prediction that the last three layers mostly dismantle.

What the lens cannot tell you

The post’s own caveat: “The logit lens show us some of what is going on, not all of it.” Three specific failure modes.

The unembedding is not the right reader for early layers. The lens assumes each layer’s residual state is in the final layer’s coordinates. Early layers write features the later layers transform before unembedding; applying the final map to them reads those features through the wrong lens, and the confident “ now” at block 7 may be a feature that later layers use for something else entirely. A low lens probability for the right answer at layer 4 does not mean the answer is absent at layer 4.

KL is not a distance. The post added this after a comment: “The KL divergence is not a metric: it is not symmetric and does not obey the triangle inequality,” so the picture of the state “travelling” from input to output along a path is a metaphor, and KL(final‖layer) and KL(layer‖final) tell different stories.

It is a look, not a test. Nothing about the lens is pre-registered. It is a way of generating hypotheses (“block 9 is where the model starts to move toward the answer”), and lesson 10 is how such a hypothesis is tested: patch block 9 and see whether the answer moves.

Direct logit attribution

The residual stream is a sum: embeddings plus every block’s attention output plus every block’s MLP output. The final logit of a token is a linear function of that sum through the final layer norm, and layer-norm centring is linear, so the logit decomposes exactly into one term per component. Each term is ((c − mean(c)) · w / scale) · u, where c is the component’s output at the last position, w and scale are the final layer norm’s weight and the normalisation scale actually used on this input, and u is the target token’s column of the unembedding. The biases of the layer norm and unembedding make one more term. The script checks that the terms add up:

Direct logit attribution to ' Paris' (each part passed through the final layer norm):
  block  0: attention   -0.04   mlp   -0.05
  block  1: attention   +0.07   mlp   -0.04

  block  7: attention   +0.21   mlp   +0.11

  block  9: attention   +1.90   mlp   +0.72
  block 10: attention   +0.48   mlp   +0.56
  block 11: attention   +0.90   mlp   +2.07
  embeddings   -0.01; layer-norm and unembed biases   +1.59
  sum of parts  +11.02; logit from the forward pass  +11.02

The sum matches the forward pass to two decimals, which is the first thing to check whenever you write attribution code; an earlier version of this script omitted the centring and the biases and was off by 1.6. Block 9’s attention (+1.90) and block 11’s MLP (+2.07) contribute most to the Paris logit. That is consistent with the lens table, where Paris first gets measurable probability after block 9. It is still correlational: a large direct contribution says the component writes in the Paris direction, not that the model’s answer depends on it, and the Paris logit here is a losing logit.

What attribution misses

Direct logit attribution measures only the direct path from a component to the output. A component whose output is read by a later block and transformed before reaching the logits contributes indirectly, and that contribution is assigned to the later block. For an answer that depends on composition (attention moving the country name, an MLP converting it to a capital), the attribution lands on the last step. The “found it in block 11” result is therefore often an artefact of where the direct path ends. Patching (lesson 10) follows the indirect paths; attribution does not.

In interpretability work

Use the lens and attribution to choose which layer and position to patch, then patch. Report lens tables as what they are: the output you would get if the model stopped early, which it does not.

Pitfall

Choosing the prompt after seeing which prompts the lens tells a clean story on. GPT-2 small fails the France prompt; the course keeps it. A clean story selected from twenty prompts is a top-k example of a different kind.

Check your understanding

The lens shows “ Paris” rising from rank 93 at block 11 to rank 1 at block 11 on a different prompt where the model is right. A colleague says block 9’s attention “retrieves the capital.” What two further results would you want before agreeing?

Reveal the reasoning

First, that the +1.90 direct contribution survives patching: replace block 9’s attention output at the last position with its value from a corrupted prompt and see whether the answer degrades (lesson 10). Second, that the contribution is specific: direct attribution on a control prompt with a different country should move to that country’s capital, and attribution to a random token should be small. Without those, “retrieves” is a description of a projection, not a mechanism.

Practice

Run the lens on the Italy prompt. Compare the rank of “ Rome” with that of “ Paris” on the France prompt, and compare which blocks carry the largest direct attributions.

python labs/reference/logit_lens_run.py
python labs/reference/logit_lens_run.py --prompt "The capital of Italy is" --target " Rome"
What to look for

Whatever the ranks are, the comparison is what matters: if the same blocks dominate for both targets, the mechanism is at least prompt-general; if they differ, you have a hypothesis for a patching experiment. Resist reading either run alone as “where the model knows capitals.”
