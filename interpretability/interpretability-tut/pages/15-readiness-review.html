Module 5 · Capstone

Readiness review

What you should now be able to do, how to check it, and the rubric for the capstone report.

The target, restated

Replicate a vision-neuron claim on InceptionV1 and a language-neuron claim on GPT-2 small, with the prediction test written before looking. Both have been done in this course with the tests in the transcript, one of them twice because the first design could not work with these weights. The question is whether you can now do it without the course.

Capstone rubric

CriterionPassNot yet
ProcedureThe neuron’s selection is written such that a reader could repeat it and get the same neuron“We picked an interesting neuron”
Pre-registrationAll eight fields filled, dated, before the held-out score; the “looked?” field is honestA field is blank, or the threshold was set after the run
Held-out dataNamed, disjoint from screening, not decoded before scoringOverlaps with the examples that suggested the claim
Score and base rateBoth reported; the score is on all held-out tokens or the regime is statedA score without the data it was computed on
ControlsAt least two, run as pre-registered, reported beside the score as percentilesControls missing, or reported as “low”
VerdictApplies the pre-registered rule; the word “demonstrated” is used only if it passedA failed threshold described as partial success
Not testedA table of what the claim does not cover, written before the results discussionOmitted, or buried in limitations
ExploratoryAnything learned after the run is labelled and separatedPost-hoc observations mixed with pre-registered ones
WordingCorrelational evidence is worded as “correlates” or “is readable”; “uses” only with an intervention“The neuron is for X” from a correlation
Revised protocolThe next test is written and labelled not yet run“Future work”

Held-out assessment

A tutor has a set of variant tasks (described, not published, in labs/ASSESSMENTS.md) that change the rule, the corpus, the stimulus family, or the model layer, so that a learner who can only reproduce the reference runs will not pass and one who can run the protocol will. That is the same principle as this whole course applied to the course itself.

After this course

The methods here are the small end of the field. Nanda’s guide and ARENA’s Chapter 1 [Nanda 2025] [ARENA] take the same tools to circuits (indirect object identification), to superposition in toy models, and to training your own sparse autoencoders. The 2026 essay and its critique are the live argument; the companion paper the essay promises, and the post Gao promises, are the next things to read with the checklist from lesson 13 open. The vision replication you can extend today: add natural images with a clear licence, run the dataset-examples line of evidence the course could not, and test the paper’s purity claim. The language one you can extend tonight: the KL test in lesson 14.

In interpretability work

Everything in evals that you learned to do for a benchmark score (name the estimand, hold out, control, correct for multiple comparisons, report the negative) applies unchanged to a claim about a neuron. Interpretability is evaluation of explanations. You already had the habits; now you have the objects.

Pitfall

Finishing the course without a failed test of your own. If every pre-registration you wrote passed, your thresholds were too easy or your claims too safe. Lesson 08’s failure is the one to imitate: a strict line, missed for a reason you can name, reported in full.

Check your understanding

Your capstone passed every rubric row and your held-out r was 0.52, just over the 0.5 threshold. A reviewer asks whether 0.52 versus 0.50 means anything. What do you say?

Reveal the reasoning

That the threshold was set in advance and the verdict follows it, so the result stands as “demonstrated at the pre-registered level,” and that the margin is small enough that a different held-out split could land on the other side: the right follow-up is a confidence interval on r from a bootstrap over held-out chunks (the statistics course’s tool), reported as exploratory, and a replication on new text as the next pre-registration. What you do not do is move the threshold or rerun until it clears comfortably.

Practice

Run the full test-suite against your implementations. Then re-read your capstone report against the rubric, row by row, and mark each pass or not yet in the margin.

python -m unittest discover -s labs -p 'test_*.py' -v
What to look for

Thirty-seven tests across six labs. If any fail, the failing test is the specification: read what it asks for, not what the reference does. The rubric rows most often marked “not yet” on a first pass are the not-tested table and the wording row.
