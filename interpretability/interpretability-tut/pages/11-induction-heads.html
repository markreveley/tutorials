Module 3 · Language methods on GPT-2 small

Attention heads: induction heads by a pre-registered test on repeated random tokens

A behavioural definition that fixes its own test data, the prefix-matching score, a non-repeated control, and what GPT-2 small’s heads do on it.

A definition that is a test

Olsson et al. define induction heads behaviourally, on constructed input: “induction heads are any heads that empirically increase the likelihood of [B] given [A][B]...[A] when shown a repeated sequence of completely random tokens,” with two properties. “Prefix matching: The head attends back to previous tokens that were followed by the current and/or recent tokens.” “Copying: The head’s output increases the logit corresponding to the attended-to token” [Induction heads]. They explain why the sequence is random: “By defining induction heads in terms of their behavior on repeated copies of random sequences, we can be confident that it's actually relying on induction rather than, say, a simple copying head.” Random tokens have no n-gram statistics to exploit; the only way to predict the second copy is to look back.

This is a definition that comes with its own held-out data: every new random seed is a fresh test. The course tests the prefix-matching property. Build BOS + R + R where R is 50 random tokens; for each position in the second copy, read the head’s attention to the position just after that token’s first occurrence; average. A head doing pure induction scores 1; a head attending uniformly over 100 positions scores about 0.01.

Pre-registered: at least one head in GPT-2 small scores at least 0.5 (the paper reports “a continuum” and sets no numeric threshold, so 0.5 is the course’s own line); every such head scores below 0.1 on a control sequence BOS + R + R′ with a fresh second block, where there is nothing to match; and the loss on the second copy is lower than on the first, which is the in-context learning the paper ties to these heads.

Reading the grid

Layers 0 to 4 are flat: no head in the first five layers attends to the induction target more than 2% of the time. Then five heads in layers 5 to 7 score above 0.84, and a looser group in layers 9 and 10 sits around 0.5. The paper’s “continuum” is visible: the scores are not two clusters but a tail, and the threshold at 0.5 cuts it at a place the course chose. Had it been 0.8, five heads; at 0.3, fourteen. Report the grid, not just the list, so a reader can move the line.

The control column is the result’s backbone. Every head that scores high on the repeat scores 0.00 or 0.01 on the control, where the second block is fresh random tokens and the “induction target” position holds nothing related. The heads are not attending to a fixed offset of 49; they are attending to a match. And the loss falls from 12.38 nats per token on the first copy, which is chance on a 50,257-token vocabulary with random tokens, to 0.43 on the second: the model predicts the repeat almost perfectly, which is what the heads are for.

The copying property was not tested. It needs the head’s output projected onto the attended token’s unembedding, a direct-logit-attribution per head of the kind lesson 09 built. It is the natural extension and the lab leaves it as practice.

Why this is the cleanest test in the course

Everything about it was fixed by the definition before anyone looked at a head: the stimulus, the score, the control, the prediction. There was no screening step, so no selection bias; all 144 heads were scored and all 144 are shown. There is no corpus, so no question of distribution shift within the data. New seeds are free. The 2022 paper is a model of making a claim testable by writing the test into the definition, and that is why the ARENA curriculum uses locating induction heads with TransformerLens as its first interpretability exercise [ARENA].

The lab

labs/induction.py asks for repeated_sequence, control_sequence (same first block, fresh second block), prefix_matching_score over an attention pattern of shape [heads, pos, pos], and the verdict. The tests build a synthetic pattern where head 0 attends exactly to the induction target and head 1 attends uniformly, and check the scores are 1 and about 0.05; they also check that a head which scores high on the control fails the verdict.

In interpretability work

When a claim comes with a behavioural definition on constructed input, you have been handed a pre-registration. Use it exactly, then vary one thing at a time (sequence length, vocabulary range, number of repeats) and report what breaks.

Pitfall

Scoring attention to the induction target without the control. A head that always attends to position i − 49 for some unrelated reason would score high on the repeat; only the control, where i − 49 holds an unrelated token, distinguishes it. In this run no such head existed, but the control is what lets you say so.

Check your understanding

A head scores 0.55 on the repeated sequence and 0.40 on the control. Is it an induction head?

Reveal the reasoning

By the pre-registered test, no: the control line was 0.1. A head attending 40% to a fixed offset regardless of content is doing something positional, and the extra 15% on the repeat may be a small induction component or noise across five seeds. Report it as a positional head with a possible weak induction component, run more seeds, and if the gap is stable, test the copying property to see whether the attended token’s logit actually rises.

Practice

Run the detector, implement labs/induction.py, then add the copying score: for each high-scoring head, project its output at each second-copy position onto the unembedding of the attended token, through the final layer norm as in lesson 09, and report the mean.

python labs/reference/induction_run.py
python -m unittest discover -s labs -p 'test_induction.py' -v
What to look for

The copying score should be positive for the strong layer-5 to layer-7 heads. If it is near zero for a head with a high prefix-matching score, that head attends to the right place but does not write the token: by the paper’s definition it is not an induction head, and your detector just became stricter than the course’s.
