Module 6 · Practice on a real service

Practice: four infrastructure incidents

For each incident, state the last successful stage, at least two plausible causes, and the next observation that would distinguish them. Then reveal the reasoning.

1. Works on the laptop, fails in CI

Hypothetical evidence: a private grader works from the developer’s laptop while connected to a VPN. CI reports name-resolution failure for grader.internal. No HTTP status or provider request ID exists. Someone suggests increasing the request deadline from 30 to 120 seconds.

Check your understanding

Where should you investigate first?

Reveal the reasoning

Name resolution in CI. The laptop may use a private DNS view unavailable to CI; CI may use the wrong resolver or be unable to reach it. Inspect the failing environment’s configured resolver and required network access. A longer application deadline does not create a missing private DNS zone. After resolution works, routing and service access still need to be established.

2. The new deployment fails at 60 seconds

Hypothetical evidence: short model requests complete. Long requests through a gateway return 504 at approximately 60 seconds. Direct backend tests can complete after 75 seconds. The client’s total deadline is 120 seconds, and there is no evidence yet about what the backend does after the gateway stops waiting.

Check your understanding

What hypothesis fits, and what evidence would you request?

Reveal the reasoning

A gateway upstream deadline is a strong hypothesis, but a consistently timed downstream failure is also possible. Inspect the gateway’s configured deadlines and termination reason, then correlate backend logs with the same request ID. The direct test follows a different path. Establish whether work continues after the 504 before adding retries or increasing deadlines.

3. Faster workers, worse results

Hypothetical evidence: concurrency rises from 10 to 100. The run finishes sooner, successful-request mean latency falls, and the reported accuracy rises from 72% to 79%. But completed samples fall from 1,000 to 620, with most missing examples containing large prompts. The report excludes all HTTP errors and timeouts.

Check your understanding

Can you conclude that the new deployment improved model performance?

Reveal the reasoning

No. The evaluated population changed and the missingness is related to prompt size. You have a conditional score over a different subset. Report counts, failure types, prompt distributions, deadlines, and retry policy. Investigate limits and queueing, then compare under a predefined population and failure-handling policy. The performance change may be mostly selection bias.

4. Healthy Service, failed requests

Hypothetical evidence: a Kubernetes Service name resolves. Its Service port is 80 and targetPort is 8000. The Pods are running, but the application log says it is listening on 127.0.0.1:8000. Requests from other Pods fail. A port-forward succeeds.

Check your understanding

How do you explain the discrepancy?

Reveal the reasoning

A loopback-only listener inside the Pod is a strong candidate: ordinary Service traffic reaches a Pod interface rather than loopback. The port-forward path differs. Check actual listener binding, ready EndpointSlices, selectors, target ports, and policies before treating the hypothesis as proven. “Running” is a process state, not evidence that the intended interface accepts requests.

Turn observations into an escalation

Impact: which runs / samples / environments are affected?
Evidence: timestamps, exact error, last successful stage, request IDs
Scope: what works, what fails, and which comparison was controlled
Hypotheses: two plausible explanations, ranked with reasons
Next test: the smallest observation that distinguishes them
Measurement: how failures and retries appear in the eval report

These cases are original exercises, not reports of real outages. The standard is not guessing the hidden answer on the first try. It is choosing a test whose result would change your belief, preserving contradictory evidence, and explaining the limits of what you observed. Revisit the diagnostic lesson if your next step is only “try again.”
