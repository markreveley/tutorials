Module 7 · Design and defend

Capstone: explain, diagnose, and defend

Your finish line is a set of things you can demonstrate. Use this review to find the next gap, then return to the relevant lesson.

The design brief

A team runs 10,000 nightly evals. Workers run in containers in a private cloud subnet. They call a hosted model API, write responses to object storage, and send completed responses to a grader. Some tasks run agents in sandboxes. Current problems include intermittent 429s, 60-second gateway failures, and an unexplained improvement in accuracy when concurrency increases. Draw and explain a revised design.

Questions to answer aloud

Check your understanding

Trace a fresh HTTPS model request from a container, then explain what connection reuse changes.

Reveal the reasoning

Identify the container network view, resolver, destination address and port, route/egress, TCP setup, TLS identity, HTTP request, gateway/backend, response framing, and persistence. A cached DNS answer or an existing connection can avoid some setup work. An explicit proxy or HTTP/3 changes the path; state your assumptions rather than insisting every request follows one sequence.

Check your understanding

Why can localhost work in development and fail after deployment?

Reveal the reasoning

It refers to the caller’s network environment. A separate container or Pod is generally a different destination. Check the intended service name, network attachment, container/Service ports, and listener binding. Do not fix a wrong destination with retries.

Check your understanding

You see a 504 at 60 seconds with a 120-second client deadline. What do you do?

Reveal the reasoning

Identify the gateway, its upstream, and their timing and termination logs using a request ID. Confirm which deadline fires and whether work continues. Align budgets to the intended request lifecycle, and account for ambiguous outcomes before adding retries.

Check your understanding

What is the difference between 30 concurrent requests and 30 requests per minute?

Reveal the reasoning

Concurrency is the number in flight at an instant. Rate concerns starts or completions over an interval, and you must specify which. Request duration relates them under stable conditions. A shared rate/token limiter and a concurrency bound solve different constraints.

Check your understanding

A model call times out. Can you claim it cost nothing or that retrying produces the same sample?

Reveal the reasoning

No. The remote service may have accepted and completed the request. Seek authoritative usage/completion information where available; otherwise record ambiguity. A retry may incur cost and produce another stochastic output. Apply a predefined policy, not an assumption of rollback.

Check your understanding

Which infrastructure does the grader need, and why does that matter?

Reveal the reasoning

If grading calls another model or database, it has its own DNS, connections, TLS, quotas, deadlines, and failure states. Measure and budget it separately. A model response can be complete while grading fails; regenerating the answer may be unnecessary and may alter the evaluation.

An observable readiness rubric

SkillReady enough to build onPractice more if…
ExplainYou can draw a request path and define each component in ordinary language.You use “the network” for every stage.
DiagnoseYou reproduce a failure, name the last successful stage, and choose a test that distinguishes causes.Your only strategy is longer timeouts or repeated restarts.
DesignYou specify flows, budgets, identities, capacity controls, and recovery states.The diagram has arrows without destinations, permission, or failure behavior.
MeasureYou account for all scheduled samples and separate attempts from logical outcomes.Errors disappear from the denominator without explanation.
CommunicateYou state evidence, uncertainty, and the appropriate component owner.You present a plausible cause as proven.

This rubric is an original self-assessment, not an industry certification or a universal hiring standard. Rehearse with a teammate or explain it into a recording. A useful answer can include “I do not know yet; this observation would distinguish the two possibilities.” Professional fluency includes knowing where your evidence ends.

Choose the next depth deliberately

If your weak point is vocabulary, revisit the request and stack lessons. If commands feel abstract, repeat the local lab and write the output in your own words. If you are moving toward infrastructure or inference-serving roles, follow the book reading map into routing, congestion control, virtual networks, and internal GPU communication. For an eval role, keep practicing the connection between infrastructure outcomes and measurement.
